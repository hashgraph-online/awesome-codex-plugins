# Research Logo And Identity

Use this reference for a logo, icon, wordmark, visual identity, or brand asset pack for a laboratory, research project, open dataset, engineering software package, portal, or commercialization-facing research service.

## Process And Scope

Use the available `brand-design-skill` for the approval-gated, image-first logo workflow and `imagegen` for raster generation or refinement. Do not bypass that workflow by creating a logo from an incomplete brief. Use this reference to supply engineering-research context, evidence discipline, and delivery QA; do not duplicate a general brand manual unless the user asks for one.

Inspect the actual project before proposing a concept: its mission, users, scientific boundary, existing name and identity, deployment surfaces, and any approved project visuals. Distinguish a research schematic or scientific figure from a logo: a logo must remain recognizable, legible, and non-misleading at small sizes without carrying the full technical explanation.

## Complete The Brief Before Generating

Obtain answers or explicit permission to use named assumptions for:

- official name, replacement versus extension of an existing identity, and required text;
- research purpose, system-level story, evidence boundary, and claims that the mark must not imply;
- audiences and context, such as scholarly, open-source, public portal, commercial SaaS, sponsor, or internal use;
- primary placements: site header, browser/app icon, repository, paper, slide, poster, social image, or print;
- visual territory, preferred and prohibited motifs, color preferences, typography, and desired distinction from peers;
- existing assets, rights constraints, accessibility needs, desired deliverables, and whether transparent backgrounds are required.

Ask only for information that materially changes the concept. If the brief is otherwise sufficient, state bounded assumptions and proceed. Do not treat source code, a project webpage, a rendered atlas, or a technical diagram as permission to reuse third-party visual assets or as evidence for an unverified scientific claim.

## Explore, Select, Then Refine

Offer several materially distinct, plain-language directions before committing to a final mark. Tie each direction to the engineering story, not to decorative symbolism alone. For coupled systems, show a legible relationship such as flow, layers, network connectivity, terrain, sensing, computation, or infrastructure; avoid generic gears, globes, leaves, lightning bolts, and neural-network imagery unless they gain a project-specific role.

Generate concept images only after the brief and user approval are complete. Require the user to select a direction before refining it. Use the selected candidate as the visual source for refinement; name the locked invariants (for example silhouette, layer count, routing, negative space, composition, or wordmark relationship) and change only the requested attributes. Do not silently replace an approved topology with a different concept. Do not reconstruct an AI-approved raster logo as an SVG unless the user expressly authorizes a separate vector-production task.

Prefer simple, bright, high-contrast forms that remain understandable as a favicon or app tile and credible in a research or commercial setting. Test color options side by side when color is undecided. Treat a project-specific palette as a preference, not a global default. For the Data Center Atlas water-energy-AI nexus, the approved optional baseline is PCB emerald `#00A878`, ultramarine `#304FFE`, and graphite `#152238`, with Manrope specified for production typography.

## Deliver A Usable Asset System

After explicit final approval, create and inspect the smallest asset set that covers the stated placements. Normally include:

- primary horizontal lockup on a light background;
- color icon without wording;
- dark-background lockup;
- monochrome icon or lockup;
- small-size/app-icon proof; and
- a concise system board showing palette, typography, clear use, and prohibited use only when the user requests it.

Use actual licensed font files in production UI, web, and editable artwork. A raster generation that resembles a font is not proof that the font was used. Preserve source files when available; label AI-generated raster assets as raster. Do not represent them as editable vectors, trademark-cleared marks, or exclusive intellectual property without the appropriate separate review.

## QA And Handoff

Inspect every final image at intended display size and on light, dark, and transparent backgrounds as applicable. Check wordmark spelling, icon-only recognizability, contrast, edge artifacts, cropping, alignment, and the legibility of fine channels or traces. Verify that the design does not make a stronger scientific, environmental, operational, or commercial claim than the underlying project can support.

Store assets in the user-specified project location using stable descriptive names. Preserve any prior approved copies. For copied or release-bound files, compare SHA-256 hashes or an equivalent checksum between source and destination. Report exact paths, formats, what was visually inspected, and residual production limits (for example, font loading, vector redraw, trademark clearance, or accessibility review).
