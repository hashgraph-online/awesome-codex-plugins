# Teaching Mechanical Engineering

Use this reference for lectures, notes, worked examples, assignments, tutorials, and student-facing explanations in mechanical and thermal engineering.

## Teaching Sequence

1. Start from a concrete engineering question, system, or observable phenomenon.
2. Define variables, units, sign conventions, assumptions, and the physical control volume or boundary.
3. Derive or state the simplest analytical or empirical baseline.
4. Work one example with intermediate calculations and physical sanity checks.
5. Connect equations to code, plots, experiments, or simulations.
6. Show a common misconception or failure mode and explain why it fails.
7. Give a transfer task that changes the geometry, regime, data, or assumptions.
8. Include a check for understanding rather than only a completed solution.

Scale mathematics and code to the audience while preserving dimensional consistency and mechanism. Use diagrams, units, limiting cases, and familiar systems to make abstractions concrete.

For computational or ML material:

- separate training, validation, and testing;
- connect features and metrics to physical quantities;
- use reproducible environments and runnable examples;
- show failure cases and uncertainty;
- distinguish a demonstration from evidence of generalization.

Respect accessibility and academic integrity. Explain reasoning and provide scaffolding without impersonating a student's work or exposing student records. Keep course-specific languages, assignments, grading, and deadlines in the project instructions rather than this skill.
