# Proposal Budget And Budget Justification

Use this reference when building a proposal budget, writing the budget justification, preparing a subaward budget, or checking that the budget agrees with the narrative. It complements [proposal-development.md](proposal-development.md). The narrative states what will be done; the budget shows that the plan can be carried out with the requested resources.

## Core Principle

Treat the budget as a second statement of the work plan. Every requested cost should trace to a person, task, deliverable, meeting, or solicitation requirement, and every number in the justification should reproduce from the institution's budget workbook and a stated basis. A budget justification is a derived document. The rates, stipends, fares, and per diem it cites are evidence with a source and an effective date, not round numbers chosen to fill a cap.

## Build Order

1. Extract the budget rules from the solicitation and the sponsor's proposal guide before entering numbers: the award cap and whether it includes indirect costs, duration, cost-sharing restrictions, required meetings or travel, lead-organization and subaward structure, participant-support rules, equipment thresholds, and unallowable categories.
2. Start from the sponsored-programs office's current budget workbook for the fiscal year, not from a prior proposal. Record the workbook version, because fringe rates, tuition, minimum stipends, and indirect-cost conventions change annually.
3. Enter personnel effort from the work plan. Tie each person's months or hours to named tasks, then let the workbook compute salary, escalation, fringe, and indirect costs.
4. Add travel, participant support, other direct costs, and subawards, each with its basis.
5. When the total must equal or stay under a cap, rebalance through real scope choices such as effort months, the number of supported participants, or trips. Recompute indirect costs after every change, because categories excluded from the indirect-cost base change the total differently from salaries.
6. Write the justification from the final workbook, then run the reconciliation checks below.

## Category Rules That Recur

- **Senior personnel.** State months per year, the salary basis, the escalation used in later years, and each person's role. For academic-year appointments, summer salary is commonly computed per month as one-ninth of the academic-year salary. Check the sponsor's salary limits; NSF normally limits senior personnel to two months of regular salary per year across all NSF awards unless a larger request is justified.
- **Students and postdoctoral researchers.** Use the institution's minimum stipend or wage with its effective date and the appointment fraction. Graduate support usually also requires tuition. If the institution budgets tuition as a direct cost but treats it as a fringe benefit or excludes it from the indirect-cost base, say so and place it on the sponsor's required line.
- **Fringe benefits.** Apply the negotiated rate for each personnel class in effect for the budget period, and note that the rates are reset annually.
- **Travel.** Give each trip a purpose tied to a deliverable or a solicitation requirement, such as a required PI meeting or a discipline meeting. When future fares are not posted, use current fares and name the source and date. Use the latest federal per diem rates, lodging taxes, partial per diem on the first and last travel days, deductions for meals provided at the event, and a mileage rate with the source of the distances. When the venue or date of a future meeting is unknown, state the assumption.
- **Participant support.** Reserve it for people outside the project team who attend a workshop or training. Give the number of participants, how they are selected, and the representative origins used for airfare. For NSF, participant support is excluded from the indirect-cost base and cannot be moved to other categories without prior approval from the program officer.
- **Workshop costs.** Venue, audiovisual services, catering, printing, and speaker honoraria usually belong in other direct costs rather than participant support. Confirm the classification with the sponsor guide and the institution, and deduct meals provided at the event from participants' per diem so that no meal is paid twice.
- **Subawards.** Each subrecipient supplies its own budget and justification, and the lead justification states the subaward's scope and annual amounts. Only the first portion of each subaward enters the lead organization's indirect-cost base. The 2024 revision of 2 CFR 200 raised that portion from \$25,000 to \$50,000, but an institution may still apply the threshold in its negotiated rate agreement or budget workbook. Confirm which threshold the sponsored-programs office uses, because the difference can move a capped budget past its limit.
- **Indirect costs.** Cite the rate agreement and its date, whether the rate is provisional, the base (for example, modified total direct costs), and the excluded categories, and report the amount by year.
- **Cost sharing.** When the solicitation prohibits voluntary committed cost sharing, keep it out of the budget and the justification, and keep quantified resource commitments out of the facilities statement and project description.

## Reconciliation Checks

Run these checks before the justification goes to the sponsored-programs office:

- Recompute each category and year total from its line items, and confirm that the category totals, direct costs, indirect costs, and project total match the workbook to the dollar.
- Recompute the indirect-cost base as direct costs minus the excluded categories and the subaward amounts above the threshold, then apply the rate.
- Check every rate, stipend, fare, and per diem against its stated source and effective date. Replace any value without a source with a marked placeholder rather than an estimate presented as fact.
- Check agreement with the narrative and the other documents: the numbers of workshops, participants, students, and trips; task assignments; months in the timeline; section and figure references; the facilities statement; and the data management plan.
- Check that the sponsor's budget-form lines, such as the separate subaward and other-direct-cost lines on NSF budgets, match the headings of the justification.

## Writing The Justification

Use one heading per sponsor budget category with its total. For each line, state the quantity, the basis and its source, the amount by year, and the project purpose in one or two sentences. Put travel and participant support in compact tables when several trips or traveler groups are involved. Keep the prose factual, because the basis and the purpose justify a cost and adjectives do not.

## Multi-Institution Proposals

Decide early which organization leads and which participate as subawards, because a solicitation may require a single lead organization with all others as subawards. A subaward investigator builds the subaward budget in their own institution's workbook at their own rates and supplies a separate justification, and the lead budget carries only the subaward total. Keep the scope statements in the lead and subaward justifications consistent with the management plan and with each other.
