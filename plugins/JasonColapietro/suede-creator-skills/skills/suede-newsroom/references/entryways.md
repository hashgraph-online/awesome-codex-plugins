# The seven entryways

An entryway is the door a reader walks through into one idea. Seven doors, one
idea, seven reasons to stop scrolling. Each entry gives the requirement, the
surfaces it survives on, the failure mode, and a worked line.

## Proof

**Argues:** this is real, and here is the thing you can check yourself.

**Requires:** an artifact a stranger can inspect without trusting you: a
screenshot, dataset, receipt, before-and-after, or public URL.

**Failure mode:** posting the artifact with a caption that describes it. The
artifact is the argument; the caption says what it settles.

**Worked line:** "Four platform posts from one campaign. Same hook, four times.
Nobody chose that: the pipeline fed the finished draft to the repurposing step."

---

## Mechanism

**Argues:** here is why it works, at the level of cause.

**Requires:** a causal chain. "X happens because Y, which is why Z" passes; "X is
important for Y" does not.

**Failure mode:** restating what the thing is. A mechanism a reader could have
guessed from the name is a definition wearing a lab coat.

**Worked line:** "A finished draft carries exactly one argument. Anything derived
from it inherits that argument. That is why your five platform posts say the same
thing: not prompt quality, not model choice."

---

## Workflow

**Argues:** you can run this today.

**Requires:** ordered steps specific enough to follow without a follow-up
question.

**Failure mode:** steps that are really categories. "Do research" is a category;
"return three to seven claims, each with a URL" is a step.

**Worked line:** the role contract published on its own, actionable with no link.

---

## Risk

**Argues:** here is what breaks, and the conditions that break it.

**Requires:** a named failure mode with its trigger. A generic caution is not a
risk entryway.

**Failure mode:** hedging. "Results may vary" is not a risk; "this degrades the
moment two roles share one memory" is.

**Worked line:** "The failure is not that the agents are bad. It is that six of
them share one memory, so every specialist has read everything and distinguishes
nothing."

---

## Result

**Argues:** here is what it produced, and the conditions that produced it.

**Requires:** an outcome with context: what was measured, over what period,
under what conditions.

**Failure mode:** the naked number. A result with no conditions invites the
reader to assume them, and they will assume the flattering ones.

**Worked line:** "Three campaigns, same evidence packet, zero rework on the last
two. The first took four returns to get the claims traced."

---

## Critique

**Argues:** the common approach is worse than it looks, and here is how it fails.

**Requires:** the actual default, what most people do, plus the mechanism of
its failure. Critique without a named default is posturing.

**Failure mode:** attacking a strawman nobody runs, or naming a competitor rather
than an approach.

**Worked line:** "Every asset in the set can be individually good and the
campaign still fails. Quality per asset is not the axis. Distinctness is."

---

## Compression

**Argues:** here is the whole shape, in one line or one image.

**Requires:** the idea reduced far enough to repeat from memory.

**Failure mode:** compressing to a slogan. A compression a reader can repeat but
not unpack is a tagline.

**Worked line:** `idea -> research -> angle -> flagship -> distribution -> review
-> performance -> playbooks`, published as the diagram alone.

## Surface fit

An entryway that survives on one surface dies on another. Consumption pattern
decides, not character count.

| Surface | Entryways that carry it | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Short text feed | proof, critique, compression, result | the scroll rewards a single sharp claim |
| Professional feed | workflow, risk, result, mechanism | the operator lesson and the before-and-after |
| Carousel | workflow, compression, mechanism | sequence and structure are clearer shown |
| Video | mechanism, result, risk | tension, demonstration, and outcome speak well |
| Newsletter | mechanism, risk, workflow | room for nuance, conditions, and context |
| Long-form | all of them, combined | the flagship is where they meet |

Where the project's own surface playbook contradicts this table, the project
wins, say which one you followed.

## Sequencing

Two rules. **Lead with the largest outcome**, result or proof opens, compression
closes, and a reader arriving at any point can work backward to the flagship.
**Space the demanding entryways**, mechanism and risk ask more of a reader than
proof or compression, so alternate rather than stacking them.

A full set in order: result, mechanism, proof, workflow, critique, risk,
compression. Critique and risk sit late because both ask the reader to
reconsider something, which lands better once the week has shown the thing works.

In a short set, drop critique and risk first: they depend on context the set has
not built yet.

## When the source is thin

Not every idea supports seven doors. An idea with no proof object does not get a
proof asset, and inventing one is fabrication.

| Inventory rows filled | Realistic set size |
|---|---|
| 2–3 | two assets, take the strongest, skip the rest |
| 4–5 | three to four assets |
| 6–8 | up to seven |

Report the unused entryways and why each is unavailable. That list is often more
useful than the set: it names exactly what the next round of research must find.
