# Suede Seven-Pass Edit

Use when the job is improving copy that already exists. One concern per pass
catches what a single mixed read misses. Every pass keeps the core message,
gives each change a reason, and keeps the author's voice. After each pass,
glance back at the earlier ones so a later edit has not undone them.

## The passes

1. **Clear.** Can a first-time reader follow it? Mark tangled structure,
   unclear pronouns, jargon, ambiguity, and missing context before you rewrite.
   One main idea per section, addressed to the reader.
2. **Voice.** Read it aloud. Mark shifts between formal and casual, "we"
   drifting into "the company", stray technical terms, and a joke that appears
   once and never again. Smooth the joins.
3. **Payoff.** After every statement, ask what the reader gets. A feature needs
   its consequence spelled out ("which means you can..."). A company milestone
   must connect to something the reader receives.
4. **Proof.** List each claim that needs support and check that support sits
   nearby: named customer results, data, third-party reviews, guarantees,
   logos. Flag "trusted by thousands" and "industry-leading" style lines.
   Claims the operator supplied about their own business stay as written and go
   to `Claims to verify`. Cut only claims an agent invented or that are
   provably false.
5. **Specific.** Replace vague verbs (improve, enhance, optimize), generic
   lines, and suspiciously round numbers with real figures, timeframes, and
   examples from the source. If a line cannot be made specific, it is usually
   filler; cut it. Never invent the number.
6. **Feeling.** Does the reader recognize the before state, the frustration
   with the old way, the relief of the fix? Add texture with one small story or
   concrete detail. No manufactured urgency, no pressure tactics.
7. **Friction.** Next to each CTA, list every reason to hesitate and confirm
   the copy answers it: trial terms, cancellation, guarantee, proof beside the
   button, privacy, what happens after the click. Replace "Contact us" with
   the real next step.

After pass 7, run passes 6 through 1 once more, quickly.

## Review bench (launch, pricing, and high-traffic pages)

Use after the passes for launch copy, pricing pages, high-traffic landing
pages, and sales pages. Optional for email and ads. Skip for small edits.

1. Seat three to five role reviewers that fit the surface. Landing page:
   conversion writer, UX writer, target buyer, brand lead. Email sequence:
   lifecycle marketer, copywriter, deliverability reviewer, target buyer.
   Long sales page: direct-response writer, skeptical buyer, editor, search
   reviewer.
2. Each role scores 1 to 10 in its area and names one specific fix.
3. Fix the lowest scores first, then score again.
4. Stop when every role is at 7 or higher and the average is at least 8.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 9-10 | Ready to publish |
| 7-8 | Strong; small fixes |
| 5-6 | Clear gaps; another pass |
| 3-4 | Major revision |
| 1-2 | Rethink the approach |

The bench is a diagnostic. It does not replace the 70-point Ship Gate in
`SKILL.md`, and the roles are simulated lenses, not real people.
