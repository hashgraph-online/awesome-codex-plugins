# Citations vs. Recommendations: The Suede Citation Ladder

Being cited and being recommended are different outcomes with different
causes. A citation means a page was useful to consult. A recommendation means
the engine put the brand on the buyer's shortlist. Conflating them leads
smaller brands into content plans that mostly help competitors.

## The ladder

| Rung | What it means | What governs it | How to see it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Retrieved | The engine read your page while answering | Crawl access, indexation, query relevance | Bot hits in server logs |
| 2. Cited | Your URL appears as a source | Usefulness of the passage: direct answer, specific facts, clear structure, freshness | Prompt panel, Bing AI Performance, GSC |
| 3. Mentioned | Your brand is named in the answer | Entity clarity and how often the web associates you with the topic | Prompt panel |
| 4. Recommended | You are on the shortlist the buyer acts on | Consensus across sites you do not own: reviews, communities, analysts, press, video | Prompt panel framing |

There is a shadow rung: **recommended against**. On requirement-heavy prompts,
engines name products to avoid and cite why. Track the framing around every
mention (recommended, neutral, hedged, against), not just the count.

## The self-ranked listicle problem

The common play: publish "best [category] tools", rank yourself first, expect
AI answers to repeat the ranking. Practitioner studies of AI Overview
citations in 2026 observed that self-promotional listicles often earned the
citation while the answer recommended other brands. The mechanism is
straightforward: the engine treats the guide as a source about the category,
extracts the competitor names and criteria you compiled, then recommends from
broader consensus, where established players dominate.

Run this as a hypothesis on your own prompt panel before investing at scale:
for the prompts where your guide is cited, is your brand recommended?

By stage:

- **Category leaders** usually get both outcomes, because third-party sources
  already corroborate them. A definitive buyer's guide shapes how the whole
  category is described.
- **Emerging brands** may win the citation and influence the category's
  evaluation criteria, which is real positioning work, but should not expect the
  shortlist from it. Weight effort toward the off-site signals below.

Useful buyer's guides still belong in the plan at every stage. What changes is
the expectation and the budget split.

## What earns recommendations

| Channel | Why it moves recommendations | Related skill |
|---|---|---|
| Review platforms and app stores | Independent validation engines treat as evidence | `suede-customer-research`, `suede-aso` |
| Analyst and industry reports | High-authority category framing that answers echo | `suede-public-relations` |
| Communities and forums | Unprompted practitioner discussion, widely retrieved and hard to fake | `suede-community-marketing` |
| Earned media | Independent restatement of your positioning | `suede-public-relations` |
| Directories and marketplaces | Category placement with consistent entity facts | `suede-directory-submissions` |
| Video and podcasts | Transcripts tie the brand to the category | `suede-social` |

The test: if an engine ignored everything on your domain, would the rest of
the web still put you on the shortlist? If not, that gap is the priority.

## What a recommendation is worth, and why attribution undercounts it

Behavioral studies published in 2026 found that an assistant recommendation is
followed by more branded searches and site visits than a passing mention, and
that most of that follow-on traffic arrives as branded search or direct rather
than as an assistant referral. Do not quote their percentages; measure your own
with the four-source method in `measurement.md`.

## Applying this

- Report the ladder, not one "AI visibility" number.
- A rising citation share with a flat recommendation share is a specific gap:
  the web does not yet corroborate your content.
- For requirement-heavy prompts, check whether engines recommend against you,
  and trace the sources they cite when they do.
