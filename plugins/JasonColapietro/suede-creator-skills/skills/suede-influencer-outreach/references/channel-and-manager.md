# Channel Choice and Manager Etiquette

## Which Channel Carries the Pitch

Pitch the contact the creator publishes for business. Check the bio link, media kit, and about page before applying any ranking.

- A DM to a creator who lists a partnerships email signals you never looked.
- A scraped email to a DM-only creator signals the same.

The ranking below only settles the leftover cases: the creator publishes more than one route, or none.

| # | Route | Effort | Value |
|---|-------|--------|-------|
| 1 | Listed manager or agent | An hour of grounding, plus relationship capital | A decision from someone who evaluates offers for a living, and a relationship that covers their whole roster |
| 2 | Listed business email | An hour of grounding plus the full structure | The channel the creator opened for business; a complete offer fits |
| 3 | DM | Near zero | Lowest: it lands next to fan mail, and the 2 to 4 sentence limit barely fits commission mechanics |

DM-first is a legitimate style when a creator lists no email, and some brand-side practitioners prefer it.

**Compliance cost runs the other way.** Unsolicited commercial DMs can break a platform's rules and draw account-level action. Cold email falls under unsolicited-contact rules: identify the sender, honor opt-outs, and in some jurisdictions hold a lawful basis for the address. A published manager listing is an invitation to pitch.

**Delete, never demote.** Drop the DM route once the creator publishes an email or a manager. Parked at the bottom, it comes back as a second parallel touch. Drop the manager route only when the creator has asked for direct contact.

**Promote the DM** when the creator publishes no business contact or their bio invites DMs. Small creators often publish neither, and they often reply most and charge least.

**B2B:** no B2B-specific channel convention exists. Use the same gate and order.

## When a Manager or Agent Is Listed

- **Treat the manager as a relationship, not a gate to slip past.** Address them, name the creator, state the fit evidence and the offer plainly.
- **Never go around a listed manager on your own initiative.** It reads as disrespect to both and can end the conversation before it starts.
- **Honor a creator's stated preference.** Survey data shows many creators with agents still prefer direct brand contact. If a creator tells you to contact them directly, do it. Never treat that as license to bypass a manager unprompted.
- **If the manager goes quiet,** follow up with the manager on the standard cadence. Do not open a parallel thread with the creator while the manager thread is live.

## Recording the Choice

Log the channel and whether a manager was involved for every pitch. Channel is one of the variables your own reply-rate history will eventually rank.
