# Examples

Original compositions that follow the first-touch structure. Names, brands, and numbers are illustrative. In a real pitch every fact comes from the user or from your own reading of the creator's work; never invent posts, numbers, or familiarity.

## B2C: Commission Offer, Direct Email

Scenario:

- Brand: a trail-running gear company selling a lightweight hydration vest.
- Creator: runs a YouTube channel reviewing budget ultramarathon kit. Lists a business email.
- Offer: 15% commission, 45-day attribution, paid on the 1st of each month.
- CTA the brand can honor: a vest on reply.

> Subject: your first-50k kit list and our vest
>
> Hi Tomás,
>
> Your "first 50k on a budget" kit list is the video I'd send any new ultra runner, and that's exactly who buys our hydration vest.
>
> I run creator partnerships at Ridgeline. Our program pays 15% on every sale through your link, tracked for 45 days and paid on the 1st of each month. At our average order that's about $14 a sale.
>
> If you're curious, reply and I'll ship you a vest to run in before you decide anything.
>
> Leah Brandt, Creator Partnerships, Ridgeline

Why it passes:

- The subject names his actual video.
- The opener is a verifiable fact framed as audience fit.
- Commission mechanics are all there: rate, window, payout date, per-sale figure.
- One ask, and it is low friction.
- No rate card, deliverable list, or contract talk.
- No other creator could receive it unchanged.

## B2B: Flat Fee, Listed Sponsorship Email

Scenario:

- Brand: accounting-automation software for small agencies.
- Creator: writes a weekly newsletter for agency owners, about 22,000 subscribers. Lists a sponsorship email.
- Offer: one sponsored issue, flat fee, budget confirmed at $3,000.
- CTA: reply for a one-page brief.

> Subject: sponsoring an issue of Agency Math
>
> Hi Renee,
>
> Your piece on why agencies lose margin between the invoice and the payout is the problem our customers hired us to fix. Your readers are the owners stuck reconciling that by hand.
>
> We'd like to sponsor one issue: your voice, your angle, our tool as the worked example. It's a flat fee and we've budgeted $3,000 for it.
>
> Want the one-page brief? Reply and I'll send it.
>
> Marcus Hale, Partnerships, Tallyroom

Why it passes:

- It names a business problem the audience has, not a feature.
- The fee is named; no rate card.
- The deliverable stays one line, with detail gated behind the reply.

## Follow-Up Plan (Fits Either Example)

| # | Day | Channel | New angle | Message |
|---|-----|---------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | 0 | Email (listed contact) | Fit and offer | First touch above |
| 2 | 7 | Same thread | Held-back proof | Two sentences: one proof point not in touch 1, then the same ask |
| 3 | 21 | Same thread | Easier ask | Two sentences: a smaller ask (sample only, brief only), then "if the timing's off, no pressure, I'll leave it here." Then honor it |

Positive reply at any touch: stop and hand off with the stated numbers unchanged. Silence after touch 3: close the file.

## The Pitch That Gets Deleted

An original composite of the documented first-message mistakes, annotated.

> **Subject: Collab opportunity with LumaSkin!! 👀**

Generic template subject plus emoji bait. The brand is in it; the creator is not.

> **Dear influencer,**

Generic salutation. The mail merge shows two words in.

> **I hope this finds you well! I've been a huge fan of your amazing content for ages.**

Filler plus fabricated familiarity. No post is named because none was watched.

> **LumaSkin is a revolutionary clean-beauty brand with five product lines, AI shade matching, dermatologist partners, and 30,000 five-star reviews.**

Feature dump. Nothing answers "why my audience?"

> **We offer very competitive compensation. Our rate card and 10-week, 8-deliverable campaign plan are attached, and rates are non-negotiable.**

Three mistakes stacked: a vague offer, a rate-card and campaign-plan dump, and hardball language in message one.

> **All content will need our standard exclusivity terms and disclosure language, per the attached contract.**

Contract, exclusivity, and disclosure terms in a cold pitch. These come after the creator agrees to talk.

> **Sign the contract, follow us on Instagram, and book an onboarding call this week!**

Three asks, none low friction.

> **Best,
> The LumaSkin Growth Team**

No named person, so there is nobody to reply to.

The two positive examples fix each of these line by line.
