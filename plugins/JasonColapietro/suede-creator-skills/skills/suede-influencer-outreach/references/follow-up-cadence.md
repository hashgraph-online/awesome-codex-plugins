# Follow-Up Cadence

Cadence is the best-sourced part of creator outreach. Independent practitioner accounts converge on the same shape: a small number of spaced follow-ups, then stop.

## Three Cadences, Ranked

Default order, best value per unit of effort first. A hard deadline or a long-term relationship goal moves it.

1. **Two follow-ups, hard stop at three messages.** First follow-up about a week after the pitch, second about two weeks after that. Captures the realistic yield from a busy creator without tipping into aggravation. Practitioners running creator programs report planning for exactly this and stopping at three.
2. **One follow-up at five to seven business days, then move on.** Near-zero extra effort and most replies land in that window. Misses the creator who was simply traveling. Best when a launch date is close.
3. **Long tail for a must-have creator.** After the standard pair, one light touch at about a month, then three months, then six. Each touch needs a genuinely new reason. Only works when someone owns the file across quarters.

By value alone: long tail, then two follow-ups, then one. By effort: the same order.

**Promote the long tail** when the user names a creator as a priority and someone will own the log. **Delete it** when nobody will. An unowned reminder fires six months later with nothing new to say.

## Follow-Up Copy Rules

- One or two sentences. Nothing from the first touch restated.
- Each one carries a new small angle: a proof point held back from message one, an easier version of the ask, or a real timing hook.
- Never "just checking in", "bumping this", or "did you see my email?".
- Low pressure by design. Silence means busy, not rejection. A guilt-trip turns a busy creator into a lost one.
- Same channel as the first touch. Switch only if the first channel was a guess and a better-listed contact has surfaced since.

## Stop Rules

- **Positive reply at any point:** stop the sequence and hand off to negotiation with the compensation facts unchanged.
- **Silence after the final message:** close the file. Reopen only with something new, such as a relevant launch or a changed offer. Never re-send.
- **A no is a no.** Log it with the reason if given, and do not re-pitch the same offer.

## B2B and B2C

This guidance is published without a business-model split. Apply the same plan to both.
