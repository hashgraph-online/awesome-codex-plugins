# Pitch Anatomy: The First Touch, Element by Element

There is no industry-standard framework for creator pitching. Published guidance is a scatter of single-outlet checklists and vendor formulations. Use the principles below; never present any one outlet's list as a standard.

## Subject Line (Email Only)

Three working patterns, best value per unit of effort first:

1. **Name a specific piece of their content.** Once the opener is grounded it costs nothing extra, and no other creator could receive it unchanged.
2. **Name the concrete offer.** Near-zero effort. Earns an open from creators already selling to their audience.
3. **Name the audience benefit.** The one every brand sends.

Keep it short enough to survive mobile truncation. No emoji. Never "Collab opportunity with [Brand]!" or "Quick question".

## Opener

One verifiable, creator-specific fact, framed as audience fit rather than flattery. The fit line states the reasoning: their audience is X, and X is exactly who buys or uses this.

Vendor studies claim specific personalization lifts reply rates. The methodology is undisclosed, so use the principle and leave the number out.

## Angle Menu, Ranked

The candidate pool for the angle brainstorm. The order is a default; the interview answers can move it.

| # | Angle | Effort | Value |
|---|-------|--------|-------|
| 1 | They already use or recommend the brand | Near zero when true | Highest. Their own behavior proved the fit; the pitch becomes "here's the paid version of that" |
| 2 | Something they launched in the last few weeks | Near zero | Good, but perishable |
| 3 | A specific piece of their content | About an hour | Strongest cold angle; passes the swap test by construction |
| 4 | A product of theirs the brand team uses | Near zero when true | Strong; the fit runs both ways |
| 5 | A natural fit with their format | About an hour | Moderate; matters most when the deliverable is unusual |
| 6 | Asserted audience overlap | Near zero | Lowest as an opener, because every brand claims it |

Ranked by value alone: already-recommending, specific content, their-product-we-use, format fit, recent launch, asserted overlap.

**Delete, never demote.** A creator who does not already use the brand deletes row 1 outright. Left at the bottom, "they love us" comes back as fabricated familiarity. Same for row 2 with no live launch and row 4 with no product the team uses.

**Promote row 6 when the data is real.** Audience overlap backed by the creator's media kit, analytics they shared, or a match against your own customer list is the second most valuable angle. Use it when the data is in hand or the offer is large enough to need a data-backed case.

## The Offer: Transparency by Compensation Model

- **Commission or affiliate.** Put the exact mechanics in message one: rate, attribution window, payout schedule, and a rough per-sale figure. A flat fee puts the risk on the brand; commission moves it onto the creator, so vague terms read as risk-dumping. Give a real number or an example payout.
- **Flat fee.** Name the model and signal that a real budget exists. Do not paste a rate card or campaign proposal. Know your number before reaching out.
- **Gifted only.** Say plainly that the offer is product or access, not payment. Professional creators often decline gifted-only offers, so make them only where the discovery work supports it.

## Deliverable Sketch

One line, kept light. A useful single-outlet checklist frames the first message as answering four questions: who you want, what you want them to do and where, when you need a response, and why the brand fits.

## CTA Menu, Ranked

Exactly one ask, low friction. Two phrasings both work: the soft ask ("would you be open to it?") and the specific ask (deliverable and terms up front). Pick by brand voice.

| # | CTA | Effort per pitch | Value |
|---|-----|------------------|-------|
| 1 | Reply for details | Near zero | A reply you can act on, nothing spent if none comes. The default |
| 2 | Sample or trial on reply | A unit of stock or an account, plus fulfillment | Highest: the product lands in their hands |
| 3 | A short call | A booked half hour and someone to run it | Surfaces the real objection fast; suits complex or multi-stakeholder B2B offers |
| 4 | Application link | Near zero | Lowest: it routes a hand-picked creator into a mass funnel |

Move from row 1 to row 2 when the product's value is obvious on contact and fulfillment needs no second approval. Move to the call when the offer is complex.

**Compliance note on samples.** A shipped sample creates a material connection when it lands, so anything the creator posts about it carries a disclosure obligation whether or not a deal follows. The other three create nothing until the creator agrees.

**Delete, never demote.** Remove any CTA the brand cannot honor this week. A "short call" with nobody to run it becomes a broken promise in the first reply. Remove the application link for any individually vetted creator.

## Leave Out of Message One

- Full rate card or pricing sheet
- Complicated deliverable lists
- Aggressive negotiation language
- Long campaign proposals
- Generic salutations
- Fabricated familiarity
- Disclosure language, contract terms, exclusivity demands. These come after the creator agrees to talk. The disclosure obligation itself is real: in the US, 16 CFR Part 255 requires any material connection to be disclosed clearly and conspicuously when the endorsement runs, and UK rules require an obvious ad label. Route that work to `suede-referrals`.

## Channel Fitting

- **Email:** subject, opener, fit line, offer, one-line sketch, one CTA. Short; every sentence earns its place.
- **DM:** 2 to 4 sentences. Fit fact, offer with the number, one CTA. No links in the first message.
- **Via manager:** same personalization as a direct pitch. Address the manager, name the creator and the fit evidence, and state the economics plainly. Managers filter on economics fast.

## B2B Adaptation

- Speak to a business need the creator's audience has, not a product benefit.
- Deliverables: webinars, white papers, articles, newsletter issues, podcast reads. Circulating podcast CPM ranges come from single outlets with no disclosed method; use them as a rough anchor only.
- Gifts take B2B shapes: extended platform access, beta invites, conference passes, hosted events. Free access still counts as a material connection for disclosure purposes.
- Most of this page comes from guidance published without a B2B/B2C split. Applying it to both is a reasonable inference, not a documented comparison.
