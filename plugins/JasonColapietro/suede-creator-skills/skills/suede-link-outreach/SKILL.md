# Suede Link Outreach Skills

Use the [prospect worksheet and vendor review](references/prospect-worksheet.md) to make inclusion and refusal decisions inspectable.

Only recommend a link where it would help a reader of an existing page. Start from the destination resource and the value it adds: a tested workflow, original finding, working utility or missing reference. Identify an exact prospect page and the relevant section rather than sending to a domain on the strength of a metric.

For each candidate, record page and observed date, audience, editorial reason, known relationship, source for independently public business contact information, and a draft-or-decline decision. Decline irrelevant pages, thin link lists, outlets that disguise paid placements, and contacts with a prior opt-out. If a relationship exists, disclose it rather than manufacturing familiarity.

Draft a concise, individual note. Explain what an editor could add or correct and why it helps the reader; link to evidence rather than requiring a reply to access it. Give the editor the choice of whether and how to cite. Do not prescribe ranking anchors, promise reciprocation, or pay and call the resulting link editorial.

If comparing vendors, request documentary evidence of publisher control, actual sponsorship labels/link attributes, itemized costs and cancellation terms. Distinguish paid advertising from earned coverage; do not give a budget interpretation, vendor suggestion or checkout link the force of purchase authorization.

Return a vetted prospect ledger, exact drafts and reasons for declines, plus a no-send marker. No automatic outreach, purchase, opt-in, follow-up or offsite link insertion.

## Assess the gap on the page

Open the resource and the proposed target. Note the concrete gap: dead source, outdated instructions, unsupported summary, or a useful step the audience now needs. Keep the exact URL and section, what the page already links to, a check date, and whether the site accepts contributions. If your source does not corroborate the claim at issue, propose an alternate source or skip. A link from a relevant FAQ can be more useful than one from a popular but unrelated homepage. Domain authority, position, or competitor backlinks can prioritize inspection; none replaces a factual editorial reason.

Match the pitch to the relationship. For an integration partner, give the relevant user journey, say what both sides can already verify, and do not imply a partnership beyond available evidence. For a maintainer's reference list, cite an exact section and explain the missing resource. For a correction, distinguish a demonstrably broken URL from a detail you merely disagree with. Do not revise contacts or rewrite as a different sender to bypass a refusal.

## Verify drafts and claims

Show one proposition per draft with a direct, accessible link and no required login when possible. If the supporting asset is private, wait for a public substitute or approved way to share it. Make attribution optional and truthfully show any affiliation. Ask for a check, not a promise of permanent placement. The draft and accepted-send state are separate; no reply, silence, or prewritten follow-up acts as permission to send. Respect suppression across an organization when recipients have already requested it.

When an operator reports a new link, verify the exact reachable source page and intended destination, effective response, link text and visible relationship attributes where possible. Distinguish a draft, a reply, a committed placement, and an observed placement. Report its actual link context and check date without equating it to conversion gain or ranking change. Use referral and conversion observations only when available; label whether attribution is direct, assisted, or unknown. If a vendor offers payment contingent on a link passing ranking signals or guarantees a position, exclude the pitch from an earned program and describe why.

## Boundaries

- Do not contact sources without explicit authorization.
- Decline paid rank-passing placements, fake citations, or invented familiarity.
