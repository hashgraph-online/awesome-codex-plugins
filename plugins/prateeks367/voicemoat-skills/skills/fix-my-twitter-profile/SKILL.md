# Fix my Twitter profile

A profile gets read by somebody who just saw one tweet and tapped your name.
They are deciding in a few seconds whether there is more where that came from.
They are not reading a CV and they are not reading in order.

Most bios answer a question nobody asked. They describe a job title, which
tells a stranger what you are paid for and nothing about what you post.

## Step 1: work out where they arrived from

The arrival changes what has to do the work:

- **From a tweet in the timeline.** They liked one thing. The profile has to
  suggest there is a pattern, not an accident.
- **From a reply.** They saw you being useful under somebody bigger. The bio has
  to explain who is being useful.
- **From search or a recommendation.** They have a problem and are checking
  whether you are about it.

## Step 2: the name field, which most people waste

It renders larger than the bio and appears next to every tweet and reply you
write. A name alone is the default and it says nothing.

This is the place for the four or five words that say what you are about. Not a
slogan. The thing you would say if somebody asked what you post about.

## Step 3: the bio has exactly one job

Say what somebody gets by following you.

Failures, in order of how common they are:

- A list of nouns separated by bullets, which reads as a search query
- Job title and company, which is a LinkedIn answer on the wrong platform
- Credentials nobody asked for, stacked
- A joke with no information, which works only if people already know you
- Keyword stuffing, which reads as stuffing to the human deciding

Test it by reading it aloud after "I follow this person because".

## Step 4: the pinned tweet is the most valuable space you own

It is the one piece of your writing you choose, rather than the one the
timeline happened to show. Most accounts either pin nothing or pin something a
year old they have stopped thinking about.

Pick the tweet you would want a stranger to read if they only read one. Not
necessarily the one with the best numbers: the one most representative of why
somebody would stay.

Check what is pinned now and say plainly whether it is still the right one.

## Step 5: the picture and the header, at the size they are actually seen

Both are small in a timeline. An avatar that is a wide shot is a grey circle at
that size, and a header with small text is unreadable on a phone.

Judge them at the size they appear, not at the size you uploaded them.

## Step 6: say what to leave alone

Name the parts that are working and why. A review that rewrites everything
usually produces a profile that sounds less like the person than it did before.

## What this will not do

It will not promise followers. A profile converts the visits a tweet earns; it
does not create them, and no bio has ever rescued an account nobody sees.

It will not stuff keywords. A bio written for a search index reads like one to
the person who arrived from a tweet, and they are the one deciding.
