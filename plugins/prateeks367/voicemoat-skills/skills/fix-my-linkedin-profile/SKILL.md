# Fix my LinkedIn profile

A profile is read in a fixed order by somebody who has already decided to be
curious. They saw a post or a comment, they clicked the name, and now they are
deciding in a few seconds whether this person is worth following.

Most profiles answer a different question. They are written as a CV, for a
recruiter, in the third person, and the visitor who arrived from a post is not
that reader.

## Step 1: work out who lands here, and from where

The arrival changes what the first line has to do:

- **From a post or a comment.** They liked one thing you said. The profile has
  to tell them whether there is more of it.
- **From search.** They have a problem and are checking whether you solve it.
- **From a message or an introduction.** They are checking whether to reply.
- **From your own link.** They were sent, and the profile is a landing page.

Ask which of these dominates before rewriting anything.

## Step 2: the headline

This is the only part that travels. It sits under your name on every post and
every comment you leave, so it is read far more often than the profile itself.

It has two jobs: say what you do in the words the reader uses, and give them a
reason to click. Common failures:

- The job title alone, which means nothing outside the company
- A row of nouns separated by pipes, which reads as a search query
- An abstraction about helping people unlock their potential
- Keywords packed in until the line stops being a sentence

Test it by reading it aloud directly after the person's name. If it does not
survive being said out loud, it will not survive a feed.

## Step 3: the first two lines of About

Everything after them is behind "see more", and most About sections open with a
warm-up paragraph nobody gets past. Those two lines carry the whole section.

They should say who you help and what changes. Not your career history, which
is what the experience section is for.

## Step 4: the rest of About

In this order, in first person, in actual sentences: who you work with, what
changes for them, the proof, and what to do next.

Proof means something checkable. A number you can source, a name you have
permission to use, a thing somebody can go and read. Adjectives are not proof.

## Step 5: featured

Three items at most, each earning its slot. Ask one question: if a stranger
read exactly one thing here, which would you want it to be? Then check whether
that thing is first.

## Step 6: say what to leave alone

Name the parts that are already working and say why. A review that rewrites
everything is not a review, and it usually ends with a profile that sounds less
like the person than the one they started with.

## What this will not do

It will not promise search placement. LinkedIn does not publish how its search
ranks people, and anyone quoting you a formula for it is guessing.

It will not stuff keywords. A headline written for a matching algorithm reads
exactly like one to the human who arrived from a post, and that human is the
one who was going to follow you.
