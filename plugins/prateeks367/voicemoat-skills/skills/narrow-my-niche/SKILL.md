# Narrow my niche

Most niche advice stops at "go narrower", which is not actionable, because it
never says narrower along which axis. People then narrow the subject, lose
interest in three weeks, and conclude that niching does not work for them.

This skill does two things the advice usually skips: it narrows along the axis
that suits the person, and it names the cost before they commit.

## Step 1: start from what they have already published

Not from intentions. Take the posts that exist, group them, and read the groups
back. Almost everyone is surprised here, and the surprise is the useful part:
people believe they write about the thing they meant to write about.

Say the split plainly. "Four of these are about hiring and the rest are about
pricing" is more useful than any positioning exercise.

## Step 2: the three axes

You can narrow by subject, by audience, or by stage. Most people only ever try
the first.

- **Subject.** What it is about.
- **Audience.** Who it is for.
- **Stage.** Where in the journey they are.

"Pricing" is broad. "Pricing for agencies" is narrower on audience and the
subject has not moved. "Pricing for agencies about to hire their first
employee" is narrower on stage, and it is now specific enough that the reader
knows in one line whether it is them.

Offer all three and let them see which one they can actually sustain.

## Step 3: the fifty-posts test

Can you write fifty posts inside this without repeating yourself, and without
research you would come to resent?

If no, it is a topic, not a niche. A topic is something you write about. A
niche is somewhere you live.

Ask for five post titles on the spot. If the fifth is a struggle, the fiftieth
does not exist.

## Step 4: name the cost out loud

Every narrowing loses people. Say exactly who.

This is the step that decides whether the choice survives. A niche picked
without naming what it gives up gets abandoned within weeks, because the first
time a post outside it does well, it looks like evidence the niche was wrong.
It is not evidence. It was always going to happen, and knowing that in advance
is the difference between a position and a phase.

## Step 5: leave a door

Roughly one post in five outside the niche, chosen deliberately rather than
whenever the writer gets bored. It keeps the account readable as a person and
it gives the restlessness somewhere legitimate to go.

## What this will not do

It will not pick for them. The axis that works is the one they can sustain for
a year, and nobody can tell from the outside which that is.

It will not promise growth. A narrower account is easier to describe and easier
to recommend, and that is the honest claim. Whether it grows depends on whether
the niche has anyone in it, which is a market question and not a writing one.
