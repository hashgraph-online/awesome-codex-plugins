# Consumer subscriptions and household bills

Checked 2026-10-29.

## Subscriptions

1. **List what they pay for** (bank or card statement, app store subscriptions, email receipts) and
   flag overlaps: two music services, three AI chats, cloud storage paid twice.
2. **Bundles they already have.** Phone and internet plans, credit cards and memberships often
   include streaming, storage or AI plans. Storage and AI bundles (Google One, iCloud+, Microsoft
   365) can replace separate subscriptions.
3. **Family, duo and student plans.** One family plan split with the household is usually far
   cheaper than several individual plans. Family plans normally require the same household: follow
   the service's rules.
4. **Annual billing** saves about 15 to 20 percent ("2 months free") for services they use all
   year. Monthly is better for anything they might drop.
5. **Rotate** streaming: one service at a time, cancel and resubscribe for the show they want.
6. **Ad-supported tiers** cost much less where ads are acceptable.
7. **Retention offers.** Many cancel flows offer a discount or a pause. Walk the cancel flow with
   the user's permission, stop before the final confirmation, and report the offer. Cancelling or
   accepting is the user's call.
8. **Free alternatives** for media and learning: public library apps (ebooks, audiobooks, films,
   courses) with a library card. See [../alternatives.md](../alternatives.md).

Lines not to cross: regional pricing through a VPN or a foreign address (services cancel these
accounts), repeating free trials with new accounts or cards, sharing plans outside what the terms
allow.

## Household bills

- **Internet, mobile and TV:** get one real competitor offer, then ask the provider's retention or
  cancellation team for the existing-customer price. Low-cost carriers on the same networks
  (MVNOs) often cost a fraction for the same coverage. Draft scripts:
  [../negotiation.md](../negotiation.md).
- **Bill negotiation services** charge a share of the savings (Rocket Money: 35 to 60 percent of
  the first year's savings). The user can usually make the same call for free.
- **Insurance:** re-quote at every renewal; loyal customers often pay more than new ones. Check
  for bundle discounts and a higher deductible if they can afford it.
- **Energy:** in markets with competition, compare tariffs at contract end; check government or
  utility rebates for efficient appliances, insulation and heat pumps.
- **Bank fees:** monthly account fees, FX fees on foreign purchases and ATM fees usually have
  free alternatives.

## Laws that help (check the user's country)

- US: the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule was vacated in July 2025, but state auto-renewal laws and
  federal rules still require clear cancellation in many cases.
- UK: new subscription contract rules under the DMCC Act are expected in 2027, not yet in force.
- EU and many other countries give a right to withdraw from online purchases within 14 days.
