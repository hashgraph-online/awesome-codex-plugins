# Asking for a lower price

Many prices are a first offer. Asking takes one message and works more often than people expect,
especially for B2B software, renewals, cancellations, bills and large purchases.

Rules for you, the agent:

- **You draft, the user sends.** Sending a message, email or chat on the user's behalf needs their
  explicit yes for that message. Show the draft, then send only if they approve.
- **Never lie.** No invented competitor quotes, fake budgets or made-up deadlines. Real quotes, real
  usage numbers and real alternatives are enough.
- **Ask for one clear thing.** A percentage, a price, a free month, or a plan change.
- **Best timing:** the last two weeks of a vendor's quarter or month (sales targets), 60 to 90 days
  before a renewal, and right before cancelling.

## B2B software and services

Levers, roughly in order of how often they work:

1. Annual prepay instead of monthly (often 15 to 20 percent, sometimes more if asked).
2. Multi-year term with a price lock (protects against renewal increases).
3. Fewer seats: remove inactive users first, ask for viewer or guest seats for occasional users.
4. A startup, nonprofit, education or open-source discount, even when no program is published.
5. A competitor's real quote, or a credible plan to switch.
6. Being a reference: a logo, a case study or a review in exchange for a discount.
7. Removing add-ons nobody uses, or moving to the plan below and adding one feature.

Price benchmarks: SaaS buying platforms (Vendr, Tropic, Spendflo and similar) publish typical
negotiated prices for big vendors. Use them to sanity-check a quote.

Draft:

```
Subject: <Product> - pricing for <N> seats

Hi <name>,

We are ready to move forward with <product> for <N> seats on the <plan> plan. Before we commit,
can you do better than <list price>? We would sign an annual term today if you can get to <target
price or percent>. <One honest reason: early-stage startup / nonprofit / also evaluating X / budget is set at Y.>

Thanks,
<user>
```

Renewal draft:

```
Hi <name>,

Our renewal for <product> is on <date>. Over the past year we used <real usage> across <N> active
seats, so we plan to renew at <N> seats. We are also comparing <alternative>. Can you hold our
current price or offer <target> for a 12 or 24 month term?
```

## Consumer subscriptions: retention offers

Many services show a discount when you start cancelling (a free month, 50 percent for 3 months, a
cheaper plan). Before cancelling for good:

1. Start the cancel flow and read every screen; stop at the offer.
2. If no offer appears, use chat and say you are cancelling because of price.
3. Never let the user accept an offer that auto-renews at a higher price without a reminder.

Chat draft:

```
Hi, I'd like to cancel my <plan> because the price is too high for how much I use it.
Is there any discount or cheaper plan you can offer before I cancel?
```

## Internet, mobile, insurance and other bills

- Get one real competing offer first (a cheaper carrier, a new-customer price).
- Ask for the retention or loyalty department, not general support.
- Ask: "What's the best price you can give an existing customer on this plan, or a plan with
  similar speed or coverage?"
- Insurance: re-quote at every renewal; loyal customers often pay more than new ones.
- Bill negotiation services take a cut of the saving (often a large share of the first year).
  Point that out; the user can usually make the same call themselves.

## Price match and price adjustment

- **Price match:** some retailers match a competitor's current price for the identical item. Check
  the retailer's written policy (excluded sellers, marketplace listings, sale events).
- **Price adjustment:** some retailers refund the difference if their own price drops within a
  window after purchase. Check the policy, set a reminder to re-check the price within the window.
- Draft: "I bought <item> on <date> for <price>. It is now <new price> on your site. Your price
  adjustment policy covers <N> days, so could you refund the <difference>?"

## Hotels, rentals and big local purchases

- Call or message the hotel directly: ask for their best direct rate, a member rate, or a free
  upgrade or breakfast instead of a lower price.
- Long stays: ask for a weekly or monthly rate.
- Cars, furniture, appliances, contractors: get three written quotes, ask each for their best
  out-the-door price, and ask about floor models, last year's model and cash or bank transfer discounts
  (check that paying without card protection is safe for that seller).

## Freelancers and agencies

- Fixed price per milestone beats hourly for clear scopes.
- Compare 3 quotes; the cheapest is not always the lowest total cost.
- Platform fees differ a lot: check what the buyer pays on top of the quote.
