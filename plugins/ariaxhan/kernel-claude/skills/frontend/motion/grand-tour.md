<motion id="grand-tour">

<mood>Cinematic, immersive, directed. The scroll is a dolly track. The visitor is the camera.</mood>

<dominant_idea>
The page is a choreographed journey: scrolling drives a continuous scene — pinned sections,
transforming imagery, a narrative with acts — rather than revealing a stacked document.
</dominant_idea>

<signature_moves>
- Pinned scenes: a section holds while scroll advances its internal animation, then releases.
- One continuous background world (video, 3D, or large imagery) that evolves across acts.
- Scroll-scrubbed transforms: scale, position, and focus keyed directly to scroll progress.
- Smooth-scroll foundation so the camera glides instead of stepping.
- Chapter transitions as scene cuts or material wipes — the journey has acts, not sections.
</signature_moves>

<prohibitions>
- No journey without a story: each act must earn its scroll length with an actual beat.
- Never hijack scroll speed or direction; the visitor holds the camera, always.
- No parallax-on-everything; depth layers serve the scene, at most two or three.
- Critical content and conversion paths never live only inside a scene — a skimmer skipping
  every act still gets the full pitch.
- Keep total journey length honest: past roughly six acts, attention is gone.
</prohibitions>

<rhythm>Filmic pacing: establish, develop, payoff. Fast beats for energy, one long held
beat for the product moment. Scroll distance per act is a directorial decision, not an
accident of content height.</rhythm>

<best_for>Product launches, flagship marketing pages, automotive and hardware reveals,
annual reports, storytelling campaigns — the one-page pitch that must feel like an event.</best_for>

<degrade>With reduced motion: acts become static full-bleed compositions in sequence — a
photo essay instead of a film, same story. Heavy scenes lazy-load below the first act.</degrade>

<danger>This is the most expensive language on the shelf — in build time, performance, and
maintenance. It exists for pages with one job done once. An everyday site wearing it is a
brochure in an IMAX theater.</danger>

</motion>
