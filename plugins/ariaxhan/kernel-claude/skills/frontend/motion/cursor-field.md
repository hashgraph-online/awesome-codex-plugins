<motion id="cursor-field">

<mood>Alive, tactile, curious. A field of matter that notices you. Quiet until touched.</mood>

<dominant_idea>
The page is a substance that responds to presence. Motion exists ONLY as response — nothing
auto-plays, nothing loops, nothing moves until the visitor does. Stillness is the resting
state; the visitor is the animation.
</dominant_idea>

<signature_moves>
- Magnetic pull: interactive elements lean toward an approaching pointer.
- Fluid, metaball, or particle trails that follow and then settle.
- Grids or fields that ripple outward from the pointer and calm behind it.
- Proximity-driven focus: nearby content sharpens or brightens, distant content softens.
- Click sparks or ripples as the only punctuation — acknowledgment, not celebration.
</signature_moves>

<prohibitions>
- No autoplaying animation anywhere, including hero loops and ambient backgrounds.
- No scroll-triggered entrances; scrolling reveals, it does not choreograph.
- No motion that continues more than a beat after input stops — the field settles.
- Touch devices get a designed equivalent (touch ripples, tilt) — never a dead page.
- The reactive layer never carries meaning; everything works with the effects off.
</prohibitions>

<rhythm>Response latency near-zero, settle time generous. The field reacts instantly and
calms slowly, like water.</rhythm>

<best_for>Portfolios, agencies, interactive studios, product pages for tactile hardware,
anything that sells craft through feel.</best_for>

<degrade>With reduced motion or no pointer: the field is simply still, and the layout must
be complete without it. Hover-dependent information is a bug.</degrade>

<danger>Cursor toys age fast when they gate content. The field is atmosphere; if removing
every effect breaks a task, the language has been misapplied.</danger>

</motion>
