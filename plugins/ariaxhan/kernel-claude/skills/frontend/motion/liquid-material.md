<motion id="liquid-material">

<mood>Sensuous, iridescent, expensive. The page is a substance. Interaction disturbs it.</mood>

<dominant_idea>
The interface is made of a material — molten metal, ferrofluid, chrome, deep water — and
every interaction is a disturbance in that substance. Content floats on or in it.
</dominant_idea>

<signature_moves>
- A liquid or metallic hero surface that deforms around the pointer and slowly heals.
- Lens-blur or refraction passing over content, as if seen through the substance.
- Metallic or iridescent fills on display type; light moves across letterforms.
- Ripple or melt distortion on section transitions; one state flows into the next.
- Specular highlights that track pointer or scroll — the light source is alive.
</signature_moves>

<prohibitions>
- No flat-minimal framing: the material needs depth, light, and viscosity to exist.
- No mechanical or glitch motion; nothing steps, ticks, or snaps — everything flows.
- No generic aurora-gradient blobs; the substance must feel viscous, not vaporous.
- The material never covers text at reading sizes; legibility floats above the liquid.
- One substance per page. Metal and water on the same surface is a materials-science bug.
</prohibitions>

<rhythm>Slow and continuous. The substance is always faintly alive — but at amplitude near
zero until disturbed. Recovery from interaction takes visibly longer than the disturbance.</rhythm>

<best_for>Fashion, beauty, audio hardware, spirits, jewelry, high-end launches — products
sold on sensation and finish.</best_for>

<degrade>With reduced motion: the substance freezes as a rich still texture; interactions
get subtle non-animated feedback. On weak GPUs, swap live shaders for a static render of
the same material — the look survives, the physics doesn't.</degrade>

<danger>This language is a performance budget hazard. One live surface maximum; measure on
mid-range mobile before committing. A liquid page that stutters reads as cheap — the exact
opposite of the intent.</danger>

</motion>
