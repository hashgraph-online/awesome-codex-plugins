<motion id="real-weight">

<mood>Playful, tangible, toylike. Objects with mass. A desk you can mess up.</mood>

<dominant_idea>
UI elements are physical things with weight, springs, and friction. They can be flung,
peeled, stacked, and dropped — and they always overshoot and settle, because mass does.
</dominant_idea>

<signature_moves>
- Sticker-peel reveals; corners lift before content detaches.
- Card decks that can be thrown, and bounce or scatter with believable momentum.
- Hung or dangling elements that swing with gravity and settle with friction.
- Elastic controls that stretch past the target and snap back.
- Gooey merges on navigation — adjacent elements attract and fuse like droplets.
</signature_moves>

<prohibitions>
- No fades of any kind; objects arrive by moving, never by materializing.
- No instant transitions; every state change travels through space with momentum.
- No shader or particle backgrounds — the physics IS the spectacle; the stage stays plain.
- No physics on text blocks or forms; reading and input surfaces stay bolted down.
- Never let the toy obstruct the task: play is optional, purchase paths are Newtonian-free.
</prohibitions>

<rhythm>Springy with real parameters: visible overshoot, one or two bounces, fast settle.
Consistent gravity across the whole page — one world, one physics.</rhythm>

<best_for>Consumer apps, games, kids' and education products, merch stores, launch pages,
brands whose personality is warmth and play.</best_for>

<degrade>With reduced motion: objects move directly to their end state with no spring; peel
and fling affordances become plain taps. The layout is a tidy desk, not a broken toybox.</degrade>

<danger>Physics everywhere reads as a tech demo. Give weight to the two or three objects
the visitor actually handles; everything else is furniture.</danger>

</motion>
