# Query Letter

A query letter is a one-page sales letter to a literary agent: 250-350
words, plain text, no attachments unless the agent's guidelines ask for
them. Save drafts to `submission/query.md`.

## Template

```markdown
---
type: query
updated: YYYY-MM-DD
---

# Query: {Title}

## Pitch

{One-sentence pitch, under 35 words.}

## Letter

Dear {Agent name — supplied by the author},

{Optional personalization line — only from facts the author supplies.}

{Hook: one or two sentences that put the protagonist, their situation, and
the pressure on the page.}

{Book paragraph 1: who the protagonist is, what they want, and the inciting
incident that sets them moving.}

{Book paragraph 2: the complication, the choice they face, and what they
lose if they fail. End on the stakes, not the ending.}

{TITLE} is a {word count rounded to the nearest thousand}-word {category and
genre}, complete at {word count rounded to the nearest thousand}, that will
appeal to readers of {Comp A} and {Comp B}.

[TODO: author to supply] {Bio: relevant credentials, publications, or lived
experience. Omit rather than invent.}

Thank you for your time and consideration.

{Author name and contact, from story.md `author` (or every name in `authors`) and `contact`}
```

## Structure

1. **Hook.** Specific, not rhetorical. Do not open with a question to the
   agent ("What would you do if...") or a dictionary definition.
2. **Book paragraphs.** Protagonist, want, inciting incident, obstacle,
   choice, stakes. Name at most three characters. Stay with the main plot;
   subplots belong in the synopsis. A query does not reveal the ending.
3. **Metadata line.** Title in capitals, word count rounded to the nearest
   thousand, category and genre (adult, YA, or MG plus genre), and one or
   two comps. Some writers put this line first; either works.
4. **Bio.** Short and relevant. A first-time novelist without credentials
   can skip it or give one line. Never write it for the author.

## Personalization

Add a line only when the author gives a real reason: the agent's stated
wishlist, a client book the author admires, or a meeting at a conference.
A generic compliment is worse than none.

## Common mistakes

Check every draft for these:

- Over 350 words, or longer than one page
- Themes and summary ("this is a story about grief") instead of events
- Too many names, invented terms, or subplots
- Rhetorical questions
- A word count far outside category norms, or unrounded (`87,342 words`)
- "Fiction novel", or pitching a debut as a series with no standalone
  ending
- Comps that are megabestsellers or decades old (see `comp-titles.md`)
- Claims about sales potential or the author's certainty of success
- A tone that does not match the book's voice
