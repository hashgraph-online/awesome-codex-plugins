# Comp Titles

Comparable titles tell an agent or reader where the book sits on the shelf
and who will buy it. Save the agreed list to `submission/comps.md`.

## Template

```markdown
---
type: comps
updated: YYYY-MM-DD
---

# Comp Titles: {Title}

| Title | Author | Year | Category | Why it comps | Verified by author |
|-------|--------|------|----------|--------------|--------------------|
| | | | | | no |
```

## Choosing comps

- **Recent.** Published in the last three to five years. Older comps
  suggest the author does not read the current market.
- **Same category and genre.** Adult, YA, or MG, and the same shelf: a YA
  fantasy comps YA fantasy.
- **Successful but not a phenomenon.** A megabestseller that everyone has
  heard of signals unrealistic expectations. Midlist and breakout titles
  work best.
- **A specific reason.** Each comp matches on something concrete: voice,
  premise, structure, tone, or audience. Write the reason in the table.
- **Two in a query.** Keep three to five in the file so the author can
  choose per agent.
- **Film and TV** can describe tone, paired with at least one book.

## Phrasing

- "{TITLE} will appeal to readers of {Comp A} and {Comp B}."
- "{Comp A} meets {Comp B}" is compact but easy to misuse: both halves must
  be recent and relevant, and the combination must make sense. Use the
  plain form when in doubt.
- "The voice of {Comp A} with the premise of {Comp B}" when the matches are
  on different axes.

## Verification

Your knowledge of publication dates, categories, and reception has a
cutoff and can be wrong. Mark every proposed comp `Verified by author: no`
and ask the user to confirm the year, category, and fit before it goes in a
query or blurb. Never state sales figures or awards for a comp.
