# Line Edit Checklist

Work one paragraph at a time. For each check, the question comes first;
the fix is a suggestion, and the author's voice overrides it. Match the
edit weight the author chose (light, medium, heavy).

## Clarity

- **Who is doing what?** Each sentence's subject and verb should be easy
  to find. Buried subjects (*It was the sound of the bell that woke her*)
  become direct (*The bell woke her*) unless the delay is the point.
- **Pronoun reference.** Every *he*, *she*, *they*, *it* points to one
  person or thing. Two same-gender characters in a paragraph is where this
  breaks; use a name.
- **Sequence.** Actions appear in the order they happen, and cause before
  effect: *She flinched when the door slammed* reads backwards at speed;
  *The door slammed. She flinched.* reads in order.
- **Dangling modifiers.** *Running for the ferry, the rain soaked her* has
  the rain running.

## Precision

- **Concrete over general.** *A bird* to *a gull*; *food* to *cold
  chips*. One precise noun beats an adjective stack.
- **Strong verbs.** Adverb plus weak verb (*walked slowly*) to one verb
  (*trudged*), when a better verb exists and fits the voice.
- **Right word.** Near-miss words (*enormity* for size, *disinterested*
  for uninterested) and words from the wrong era or world.
- **Figurative language.** Each metaphor comes from the POV character's
  world, fits the tone, and does not mix with the one beside it. Cut
  figures that explain what the plain sentence already showed.

## Economy

- **Redundancy.** *Nodded her head*, *shrugged his shoulders*, *stood up*,
  *sat down*, *thought to herself*.
- **Throat-clearing.** *She began to*, *started to*, *seemed to*,
  *managed to*, unless the incompleteness matters.
- **Stacked hedges.** *Almost*, *just*, *a little*, *somewhat*, *kind of*.
  Keep them in dialogue where they are characterisation.
- **Stage directions.** Movement the reader does not need (crossing the
  room, turning to face) between two lines that matter.
- **Double telling.** An emotion named after the action already showed
  it. Cut the naming.

Do not cut for its own sake. A light edit trims only repetition and
clutter; lush or maximal voices keep their length.

## Rhythm

- **Sentence length follows intent.** Short for impact, long for flow or
  overwhelm. `story prose .` flags uniform spread; fix by intent, not by
  alternating lengths mechanically.
- **Paragraph endings.** The last words of a paragraph carry weight. End
  on the image or the turn, not on a trailing clause.
- **Echoes.** Repeated words within a few lines (the prose report lists
  them). Keep deliberate repetition.
- **Sound.** Accidental rhyme, alliteration, and tongue-twisters; the
  read-aloud pass catches these best.

## POV and distance

- **Filter words.** *She saw*, *heard*, *felt*, *noticed* put a pane of
  glass between reader and scene. Remove them where the perceiving is not
  the point. See `../../voice-style/references/prose-checks.md`. The
  word list is English; in another language, look for the same effect
  (verbs of perceiving that announce instead of show) by reading.
- **Head-hopping.** Only the POV character's thoughts and feelings are
  known directly; others' show through behaviour.
- **Knowledge slips.** The POV character names things they could not
  know or would not notice.
- **Tense and person.** Consistent with `story.md`'s `tense` and `pov`.

## Voice

- **Consistency with the Voice section of `style-sheet.md`.** Distance,
  register, rhythm, and what the prose never does.
- **Genericness.** Stock phrases (*a beat of silence*, *let out a breath
  she didn't know she was holding*) replaced with the specific thing this
  character would notice. Add real tics to `watch-words`.
- **Don't flatten.** Fragments, dialect, run-ons, and odd syntax that are
  part of the voice stay. When in doubt, ask.

## Character-voice differentiation

When `story voices .` reports two voices near-identical, or dialogue
reads interchangeably, use these levers, one or two per character, drawn
from their character file:

| Lever | Examples |
|-------|----------|
| Sentence length and completeness | Clipped fragments vs full, subordinate-heavy sentences |
| Contractions and register | *I will not* vs *I won't* vs *'m not* |
| Vocabulary domain | Trade, class, region, era, education; what their metaphors come from |
| Questions vs statements | Deflects with questions; never asks one |
| Signature words (`voice-words`) | A pet phrase, oath, or verbal habit used sparingly |
| Forbidden words (`voice-avoid`) | Never swears; never says *sorry*; never uses a name |
| What they avoid talking about | Changes the subject, jokes, goes silent |
| Directness | Says what they mean vs talks around it |

Test: cover the tags on a page of dialogue. The author should be able to
tell who is speaking from the line alone for the main cast. After edits,
rerun `story voices .` to confirm the fingerprints moved apart.
