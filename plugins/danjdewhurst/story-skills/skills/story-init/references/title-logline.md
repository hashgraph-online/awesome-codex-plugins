# Title & Logline

Craft guidance for naming the story and pitching it in one sentence.

## Title Craft

- **Comps:** A working title should sit comfortably beside comparable titles in the genre — same weight, same register. If every comp is a single evocative noun and yours is a full clause, it signals the wrong shelf.
- **Hook phrasing:** The best titles carry a small hook — a contradiction, an image, a question the premise raises. One unexpected word beats a paragraph of description.
- **Title as promise:** The title is a contract with the reader about tone and subject. A grim title on a light romance (or the reverse) breaks the promise before page one. Check the title against the `premise:` in `story.md`: do they promise the same story?

Test the title out loud, in a search box (is it taken?), and against the chapter-one content. Rename freely during planning; rename reluctantly after chapter five.

## Logline Recipe

One sentence: protagonist + want + obstacle + stakes.

> {Name}, a {protagonist description}, wants {goal} but {obstacle} — and if they fail, {stakes}.

- **Protagonist:** a vivid fragment, not a resume. "A disgraced cartographer" beats "John, 34, from Ohio."
- **Want:** the external goal — what they are chasing across the whole story.
- **Obstacle:** the force opposing them (person, system, or circumstance), concrete enough to picture.
- **Stakes:** what is lost if they fail — specific and personal, not "the world" unless the world is genuinely at stake.

The logline is a planning tool, not marketing copy. When the draft drifts, compare it to the logline: either the drift is the story finding itself (update the logline), or the story lost its thread (steer back).
