**Rule**: Treat shared references as patterns to borrow, not specs to copy. Borrow only from the chosen direction, and replace their content with the product's own.

**Evidence**: Most shared app designs (Dribbble, Behance, social posts, AI-generated concepts) are onboarding or marketing shots, often never built. They show ideal data, no edge cases, and no daily screens. Their social proof ("Loved by 10k+ users", ratings, avatars) is placeholder.

### When Building

- **Name the pattern, then generalise it.** "Two-line heavy headline with one accented word" or "real UI tiles rotated behind the headline" can move to another product. A specific layout or illustration cannot.
- **Borrow from one direction.** A Character-led mascot next to a Restrained finance hero looks like two products. Choose the direction first (Step 3 of the skill), then use only references that fit it.
- **Replace their imagery with the product's content** (`rules/imagery.md`). The reference's pastel 3D shapes become the product's real interface, data, or subject matter.
- **Separate showcase from daily screens.** References show the loudest version of a direction. Daily screens use the same tokens at a lower volume.
- **Leave placeholder social proof behind.** Show ratings, user counts, and testimonials only when they are real and current.
- **Note what references get wrong**: spelling mistakes, mislabelled buttons, unreadable text over images, contrast failures. Do not copy those.
- **Never reproduce another product's distinctive identity**: its logo, mascot, signature illustration, or exact layout.

### When Auditing

- Several reference directions mixed on one screen or across a flow
- Showcase-level decoration (rotated tiles, doodles) on daily screens
- Copied illustration or placeholder content that does not belong to the product
- Fake or unverified social proof
- A screen recognisable as a copy of a specific other product

### Incorrect

```text
Match reference 3 exactly: same mascot, same "Join 50k+ happy users" line,
same purple gradient.
```

### Correct

```text
From reference 3: heavy two-line headline, one accented word, the ink
pill button pinned to the bottom. Our own content replaces the mascot;
social proof is left out until we have real numbers.
```

### Why This Matters

References are the fastest way to agree on a look, and the fastest way to ship something generic or misleading. Borrowing patterns keeps what makes a reference appealing while the product stays recognisably its own and honest.

### Alternatives

When the user explicitly wants to match a reference closely (a client mockup, a design file to implement), follow it. The point is implementation, not inspiration. Still replace placeholder content and fix obvious errors.
