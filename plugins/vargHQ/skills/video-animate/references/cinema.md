# Cinema: which masters' tricks to borrow, and what each asks of the model

Decides which technique to use on a shot, how to check it in the previz or prompt, and how much it risks with current
video models. Shot sizes, lens to FOV, basic moves, the 180 line and the 30 degree rule are in the `threejs-animatic`
skill (`references/camera-language.md`) and are not repeated.

Legend: `[vendor]` = company selling or reselling it, or a paid partner; `[self]` = one person's own report; `[measured]` = varg's own measurement, usually one film; `[inference]` = our reasoning, nobody tested it; no tag = opened primary source (docs, standard, paper, book).

**Every check line, prompt line, threshold and AI risk rating here is our own translation and untested
`[inference]`.** Only the craft and the cited numbers come from sources; "why" paraphrases the linked source unless
tagged; `~` is a starting value we chose. Video essays (EFAP = Every Frame a Painting), Wikipedia and blogs carry no
tag because they were opened, but they are secondary: an essay is a critic's reading unless it quotes the filmmaker.
Essay times are positions in the essay, within about 30 s. Captions are often auto-generated and some pages came
through a summarizing fetch tool, so check a quote's wording before repeating it in public. Never put a living
filmmaker's name in a generation prompt: names here are for teaching.

## What the models measurably get wrong

| Ask | Measured | Do instead (ours, untested) |
|---|---|---|
| Moving camera on an action prompt | Action against dialogue prompts, nine-model text-to-video mean: camera movement -31.1 points, focus -28.6, shot scale -26.6, composition -21.0, physical plausibility -16.9 ([FilmBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24241)) | Lock the camera at impact, move before or after |
| Several shots in one prompt | Single to multi-shot, nine-model average: editing fluency 97.3 to 67.5, focus 91.7 to 68.7, camera movement 87.0 to 65.1, viewing angle 88.6 to 74.3; overall -7.9, up to -22.8 for the weakest ([FilmBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24241)) | Generate shot by shot when framing or camera matters |
| Any camera movement | Highest variance of any sub-metric: Seedance 2.0 leads at 86.5, others reach down to the low 50s; cross-model mean 69.5 on text, 55.6 on reference, against tone and colour at 95.0 and 91.2 ([FilmBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24241)) | One simple move per clip, top models only; put the craft into light and blocking |
| Zoom | On Veo 3.1, 4 s clips, manual check: zoom instructions often gave static shots or dolly moves ([arXiv 2607.03043](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.03043)) | Do it in post as a scale or crop |
| Arc, roll, fast rotation | Same test: arc and clockwise roll were hard; many clockwise rolls came back counterclockwise. Error rises with camera angular velocity, typical of intense fight shots ([CineTechBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15145), May 2025, models now old) | Arc: pass the previz as reference. Roll: check direction, flip in post if wrong. Whip or spin: add the blur in post |
| A previz on Seedance 2.5, one of our films | Cuts within one frame, statics solid, 3 of 6 measurable pushes matched, the one orbit not reproduced `[measured]`, one film; see [post-ready.md](post-ready.md) | Trust cuts and statics; measure every push and orbit against the previz |
| Dolly zoom, rack focus, stated focal length | No dated test isolates them by model: the top gap here. FilmBench's focus metric is the nearest number for rack focus and is not isolated. A guide says models recognise "dolly zoom"; a tip, not a test ([Atlabs](https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/ultimate-prompt-guide-best-camera-movement-prompts-for-ai-videos-2026) `[vendor]`) | Dolly zoom: test first. Rack focus: one per clip, camera locked, or in post from a depth matte. Focal length: real distance and FOV in the previz, the look in words |

FilmBench ran Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 and others, not Seedance 2.5 or Kling 4; no public benchmark of those
turned up. Its authors include Alibaba addresses and the Beijing Film Academy, and it tested HappyHorse; we did not
confirm who owns HappyHorse, so treat that as a possible conflict.

**Takeaway: most signature moves ask less of the camera, so they lower AI risk `[inference]`.** Six ideas recur across
masters who never coordinated: decide what a scene is about before placing the camera, move actors before the camera,
ration close-ups and moves so they mean something, lead the eye, layer depth, give emotion time.

## Staging and blocking

| Technique | Master, exact example, link | Why | Check (ours, untested) | AI risk (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| One sentence for the scene | Soderbergh, as captioned in the essay: a scene can hold many things but is about one, here "a guy in the cart" ([EFAP 1:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wa1O3nqW5hQ&t=90s)) | The shot follows once that is decided | `shot.about` is one noun phrase; flag neighbours that differ with no story reason | Low, a decision |
| Size equals importance | "Hitchcock's rule", see warnings. Common wording: "The size of an object in the frame should equal its importance in the story at that moment" ([Gurney Journey](https://gurneyjourney.blogspot.com/2013/08/hitchcocks-rule.html)) | Decides how tight to frame and when to cut close ([No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/11/hitchcock-rule-help-you-tell-better-visual-stories)) | Largest element in frame is the subject named in the beat; a prop that matters at beat N is not the largest before N. Frame one size tighter (camera-language.md) | Low |
| Close-up rationing | Fincher: "Every time you go to a close-up... the audience knows: Look at this, this is important" ([EFAP 2:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QPAloq5MCUA&t=171s)). Se7en office: the chief's only close-up comes when Mills is dismissed ([4:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QPAloq5MCUA&t=273s)) | Withheld elsewhere, the shot gains power ([3:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QPAloq5MCUA&t=196s)) | At most 2 CU or ECU per scene, each with a one-word reason | Low |
| Bomb under the table | Hitchcock's own account: a surprise bomb gives "10 seconds of shock"; told first, the audience works for five minutes; "the bomb must never go off", his stated error in Sabotage ([AFI clip 0:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DPFsuc_M_3E)) | Information changes the same dull footage, and the audience needs relief | Threat insert before the talk, a visible clock, cut back ~every 10-15 s, end in relief. Prompt: `Insert, locked off: a timer taped under the table reads 4:59. Camera does not move.` | Low, static inserts |
| Kuleshov three-shot | Kuleshov as reported by Pudovkin: the accounts differ and no documentation is known to survive. Hitchcock demonstrated it on CBC's Telescope, 1964 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuleshov_effect)). Lab form: face 1.5 s, scene 3 s, same face 1.5 s; fear contexts shifted ratings, happiness did not differ from neutral ([Calbi 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/article/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01684/full)) | A neutral face reads as what it is cut against; studies disagree (Prince and Hensley 1992 found no effect, Barratt 2016 and Mobbs 2006 support it) | Face 1.5 s, object 3 s, same face clip 1.5 s, three-quarter profile, eyeline to the object's side. For fear or threat, not joy. Prompt: `Neutral expression, eyes off-camera left, no change of expression, breathing only.` | Low, one face clip used twice |
| Eyeline as point of view | Silence of the Lambs, first meeting: both look into the lens as equals, then the looks swap when he becomes interested ([EFAP](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5V-k-p4wzxg)). The Imposter: only the villain looks into the lens ([EFAP 0:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y0TnU80idDA&t=31s)) | Closeness to the lens decides whose head we are inside | Viewpoint character's gaze within ~5° of the lens axis, the other within ~15° but not on it. Prompt: `She looks directly into the lens and holds it. Camera at her eye height, locked.` | Medium, gaze unmeasured, check by eye |
| First camera move on the reversal | Silence of the Lambs: the scene's first dolly comes at the "rube" line and ends with Clarice short-sided; the second comes with the first two-shot ([EFAP 1:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5V-k-p4wzxg&t=91s)) | The one move marks the power flip | Every shot before the reversal beat has START equal to END | Low, removes most camera risk |
| Height blocking | True Grit: 1969 sets the two at equal height on the stairs; 2010 has Mattie at the bottom so he towers, yet she stops him wherever he moves ([EFAP 2:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wa1O3nqW5hQ&t=150s)) | Power shows in the staging, with no camera move | Dominant eyes ~0.3 m higher after the power beat, equal or reversed before. Prompt: `He stands two steps above her, she looks up at him.` | Low with a previz, a static pose |
| Stations in one master ("Spielberg oner") | Raiders bar: one 90 s take holds a push-in, two matching singles and an insert; Jaws' ferry barely moves the camera and uses the background ([EFAP 0:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8q4X2vDRfRk&t=51s)). Rules: move actors, follow them, break into compositions, shoot a cutaway, keep it short ([5:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8q4X2vDRfRk&t=316s)) | Several compositions in one take, the move unnoticed | ~2 stations per 5 s clip; camera moves only between stations and only when an actor moves; or a static master with moving actors | High for long takes, medium for two stations |
| Alternate the axis ("L system") | Wolfcrow's reading of Spielberg: shot N moves in depth, shot N+1 sideways, actors turn 90° within a shot; Munich named as the film to study ([Wolfcrow 0:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xu6v0nGUl64&t=35s)) | Variety from simple moves `[inference]` | Axis from the angle between travel and view: depth under ~30°, sideways over ~60°; flag two same-axis moves in a row unless one is static | Low, each shot stays simple |
| Lockstep camera | Nerdwriter's reading of Fincher, not Fincher's statement: "have the camera exactly match the velocity and direction of the moving character" ([Nerdwriter 1:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GfqD5WqChUY&t=63s)) | Locks the viewer into the character's behaviour, Nerdwriter argues | Camera moves only while the subject moves, start and stop within ~2 frames, eyes vary under ~3% of frame height. Prompt: `As she stands, the camera tilts up at the same speed and stops the moment she stops.` | Medium, check by tracking |
| Screen direction as meaning | Snowpiercer: camera left is the back of the train, right the front ([EFAP 0:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X05TDsoSg2Y&t=54s)). Wright's travel montage: the lead always faces forward or right ([EFAP 2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3FOzD4Sfgag&t=129s)) | Left and right carry the plot | Record a film-wide direction (right = forward); flag any reverse with no story reason | Medium, verify with optical flow, see below |

## Lens and light

| Technique | Master, exact example, link | Why | Check (ours, untested) | AI risk (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide lens inside the talk | Coens: camera between the speakers, close, on 27 or 32 mm; a voice after the mention of Deakins: "there's a sense of presence" ([EFAP 1:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5UE3jz_O_EM&t=108s)) | Uncomfortable and funny, exaggerates the face and moves toward camera, keeps the room readable ([2:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5UE3jz_O_EM&t=163s)) | Real distance in the previz, not "27mm" in text. MCU, 0.5 m frame height, 16:9: 0.67 m on 27 mm, 0.79 m on 32 mm; times 0.56 for 9:16 | Medium, adherence unmeasured |
| Track-away | Taxi Driver slides away from Travis on the phone to an empty corridor, unmotivated; Paths of Glory uses the lateral for the extent of the trenches ([EFAP 3:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pdSKot0psNg&t=215s)) | The lateral is among the least subjective shots; here it makes us feel bad for him | Subject leaves frame before the end; hold ~2 s on the empty frame. Prompt: `The camera slides sideways away from him along the wall until only the empty corridor remains.` | Medium, a simple truck |
| Few lenses | Deakins favours mid to wide primes, such as 32, 35 and 40 mm, and avoids zooms without a specific reason ([In Depth Cine 4:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pb4J-KoV7HU&t=293s)). 1917: 40 mm mostly, 35 mm in tunnels, 47 mm in the river (American Cinematographer via [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_(2019_film))) | One consistent space, and no zoom to fail `[inference]` | At most ~3 focal lengths per film, each change with a reason; no zoom unless named | Low |
| Layered tone | Deakins: lighting in layers, such as a bright foreground, dark middle ground and light background, gives depth ([In Depth Cine 7:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pb4J-KoV7HU&t=462s)) | Depth from tone, not from the camera | Three planes differ visibly in brightness, on the look reference, since the previz stays flat. | Dolly zoom | Devised by Paramount second-unit cameraman Irmin Roberts for Vertigo, not by Hitchcock ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolly_zoom)); explainer at [In Depth Cine](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TxiQMkSvPDM) | The subject keeps its size while the space changes | Short hero shot via the previz, or fake it with a post scale on a matte; test before promising it | High, untested |

## Hiding cuts and joining clips

| Technique | Master, exact example, link | Why | Check (ours, untested) | AI risk (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Motion onset after the cut | Removing motion after a match-action cut made viewers spot it faster and miss it less; removing motion before it had no effect ([Smith and Santacreu 2017](https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/14904/)). About a quarter of same-scene edits went unseen, about a third with sudden motion onset ([Smith and Henderson 2008](https://bop.unibe.ch/JEMR/article/view/2264)). Kurosawa "tends to cut on movement" ([EFAP 3:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doaQC-S8de8&t=209s)) | The incoming motion hides the cut | In-clip motion starts within ~6 frames of the cut; generate a head start and trim. Prompt: `She is already turning at 0.0 s.` | Low, a trim |
| Unbroken audio | "Audio played a surprisingly large part in creating edit blindness" (Smith and Santacreu, [2017](https://eprints.bbk.ac.uk/14904/)); edit blindness mostly disappeared without audio | Continuous sound helps the cut go unseen | Music or room tone runs through every cut; none in digital silence unless the jolt is meant ([sound-sync.md](sound-sync.md)) | Low, free with locked audio |
| Sound overlap, split edits | Dede Allen "would overlap sound from the beginning of the next shot into the end of the previous shot" (Greg S. Faller, in [MUBI Notebook](https://mubi.com/notebook/posts/dede-allen-1923-2010)). L cut: picture cuts first, audio continues ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_cut)) | Quickens rhythm, connects scenes ([CineMontage 2007](https://cinemontage.org/?p=23903)) | Next scene's sound ~8-24 frames before its picture, or the voice runs ~8-24 frames over the listener | Low, post only; can hide lip-sync trouble |
| Eye trace | Nedomansky: an arrow crosses one shot, then the target with the arrow already in it ([Vashi Visuals](https://vashivisuals.com/?p=5288)). Murch ranks it fourth; right emotion and story cover problems with it ([StudioBinder 9:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PKYeClvvlTw&t=561s)) | The eye follows a natural path over the cut | Focus point out and in, per cut: under ~15% of frame width apart for a hidden cut, over ~40% only for a deliberate jolt | Low, pick takes, trim or crop |
| Crosshair framing | Fury Road: nearly every shot centre framed so viewers do not hunt for the action; 480+ hours became 120 minutes and 2,700 shots. "Put the cross hairs on her nose!" is Miller as reported by [Nedomansky](https://vashivisuals.com/?p=5288); [Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2015/06/05/filming-mad-max-fury-road-with-dp-john-seale-acs-asc-and-2nd-unit-cinematographer-david-burr-acs/) holds no direct Seale quote on it | Very short shots still read | Under ~2 s, focus point inside the central ~20% box. Prompt: `Keep the driver's nose exactly centered in frame for the whole shot.` | Low, suits 9:16 |
| Foreground wipe | Rope ends 5 takes on a dark jacket or furniture back ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rope_(film))). Jaws' beach: three shots get closer as a passerby crosses ([Wolfcrow](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OR0TKRiKBQs)). Kon wipes with objects ([EFAP 1:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oz49vQwSoTE&t=110s)) | The join happens while the frame is covered | Occluder covers ~90%+ for ~2+ frames at the end of A and start of B; dark, low detail, both shots moving the same way; or composite it | Medium in generation, low in post |
| Axial cut-in | Wolfcrow: "it must feel like you cropped the first image to get the second"; Jaws' Brody, Ikiru's hat ([0:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OR0TKRiKBQs&t=31s)). Breaks the 30 degree convention on purpose | Impact, or anxiety with little jolt, per Wolfcrow | B is a crop on A's axis, ~1.5x or more; add an explicit axial flag so the 30 degree lint passes it | Low, one generation |
| Impact overlap | Jackie Chan: the hit lands in the wide and starts again a little earlier in the close. Tony Zhou's narration, not Chan: "These 3 frames are for the audience's eyes to register the new shot." ([EFAP 5:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Z1PCtIaM_GQ&t=309s)) | The mind makes one stronger hit from two | B starts ~3 frames before the contact shown at the end of A | Low, a trim |
| Match cut | Satoshi Kon: general match, exact graphic match, mirrored time periods; Paprika's first four minutes link five dream sequences this way ([EFAP 1:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oz49vQwSoTE&t=81s)) | One image hands over to the next `[inference]` | Silhouette overlap of A's last and B's first frame ~0.6 IoU; set B's first frame in the previz to A's last | Low with first-frame input |
| Whip pan | A pan so fast the picture blurs, a transition much like a natural wipe ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_pan)) | Blur covers the join `[inference]`; the page does not say it hides cuts | Directional blur on the last ~6-8 frames of A and first ~6-8 of B, same direction, in post, not in the prompt | High if prompted, low in post |
| Cutaway insurance | Spielberg often shoots an insert for a oner even when sure it worked ([EFAP 6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8q4X2vDRfRk&t=392s)). Schoonmaker built Raging Bull's improvised kitchen from single-camera singles ([clip 1:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6enMrxbpI-w&t=79s)) | Lets you tighten and join two takes | Every clip over ~5 s has one approved insert or reaction in the plan | Low, inserts cover drift |

**Hide a join, cheapest first `[inference]`;** stop as soon as the cut stops showing:

1. Keep audio running through the cut.
2. Trim so the in-clip starts on motion.
3. Match focus position within ~15% of frame width, by take choice, trim or crop.
4. Only if it still shows, add a designed joint: impact overlap, then match cut, then wipe, then whip blur.

`transition-craft` and `hyperframes-keyframes` can run the wipe, axial crop and blur in post. Decide join frames up
front: pass approved frames as first and last frame where the model takes them. FramePack scored CamMC 95.62 with both
ends against 208.78 with the first only, lower is better ([CineTechBench, Table 4](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15145),
column order read from page text). All edit-blindness data is live-action film, untested on AI clips that drift
between shots. "let an action that starts in one shot finish in the next" (camera-language.md) stays for story
clarity, but the 2017 data says outgoing motion is not what hides the cut: worth one A/B test `[inference]`.

## Making a shot feel alive in 2 to 3 seconds

| Technique | Master, exact example, link | Why | Check (ours, untested) | AI risk (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Five kinds of motion | Kurosawa: nature, groups, individuals, camera, the cut ([EFAP 1:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doaQC-S8de8&t=78s)); not all in every shot ([7:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doaQC-S8de8&t=422s)). Seven Samurai's banner scene against an Avengers dialogue scene where only camera and one actor move ([4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doaQC-S8de8&t=264s)) | Motion in the frame, not only from the camera | 2 or 3 kinds per shot; a still shot of people still needs nature motion. Prompt: `Wind moves the banner and the grass throughout, even while the men sit still.` | Low, ambient motion is forgiving |
| One moving thing leads the eye | There Will Be Blood's map shot: a raised hand drew all eyes, the map got few fixations unless gestured at. A pilot of 11 viewers, which Tim Smith calls exploratory ([Bordwell's blog](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2011/02/14/watching-you-watch-there-will-be-blood/)) | A single shot can steer attention as well as cutting, Smith argues | Name one moving element per beat, the rest stay still or small. Prompt: `Everyone stays still. Only his right hand rises.` | Low |
| Entering the frame | Wright: enter frame, leave frame, there and back again ([EFAP 3:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3FOzD4Sfgag&t=233s)) | Sudden appearances and motion on a still ground predict where everybody looks ([Tim Smith](https://www.davidbordwell.net/blog/2011/02/14/watching-you-watch-there-will-be-blood/)) | An entrance into a still frame within the first ~1 s. Prompt: `The frame is empty for 0.5 s. A hand slams in from the bottom edge.` | Medium, timing not guaranteed |
| Stillness, then motion | Kurosawa ends a scene on something static, then cuts straight into movement ([EFAP 3:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doaQC-S8de8&t=209s)) | You cannot guess the next cut | Last ~12 frames of A have no subject motion, first ~6 of B have strong motion | Low |
| Layered parallax | Michael Bay: Battleship's copy of his hero shot fails, "without a background we don't get parallax" ([EFAP 1:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2THVvshvq0Q&t=79s)). Bad Boys: car one way, plane another, lamp posts for scale, on a telephoto ([2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2THVvshvq0Q&t=158s)). Critique: the same move whether the talk matters or not ([3:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2THVvshvq0Q&t=185s)) | Planes moving at different speeds sell scale | ~3 depth planes, a scale reference, a simple truck past the foreground. Prompt: `Low angle. He stands up as the camera slides past a line of lamp posts in the foreground, with trucks far behind.` | High for a full arc, medium for a truck |

**Safe 2 to 3 second shot `[inference]`:** one clear onset in the first ~6 frames, one moving element, ambient nature
motion, a foreground plane if the camera moves, one simple move or none. No source measured "feels dynamic" in a
window this short.
Prompt (ours): `Camera locked. Wind moves the grass. Only his right hand moves, already rising at 0.0 s.`

## Escalating across a sequence

| Technique | Master, exact example, link | Why | Check (ours, untested) | AI risk (ours) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shot-length ramp | Empire Strikes Back, Luke lifting the X-wing: shots shorten to the peak, then hold longer than on the way up; about 15 s up, twice that down ([EFAP 3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Q3eITC01Fg&t=187s)) | Speed going in, time to feel it after | Durations fall to the peak; time after it ~2x or more; plan clips with handles | Low, an edit decision |
| Emotions take time | Same essay: Scott's failure in a recent film takes 30 frames, Luke's 30 seconds ([4:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Q3eITC01Fg&t=273s)). Schoonmaker: "Sometimes hold just a little bit too long... and then make a cut, if it's justified" ([7:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Q3eITC01Fg&t=435s)) | The cut waits for a change in the actor's eyes ([1:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3Q3eITC01Fg&t=84s)) | Face holds ~2+ s after the beat; generate ~1 s head and tail handles | Medium, a long hold exposes the face |
| Suspense pattern | Wolfcrow's analysis of Hitchcock, a critic's label: camera moves before and after the action, near static during it, long shots to close-ups, release in a long shot from another angle; Arbogast's stairs, Vertigo's tower ([Wolfcrow 6:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T7gfcP08fOA&t=368s)) | A still camera leaves the action as the one motion `[inference]` | Moves before and after; action shots have START equal to END; release has a new angle; WS, MS, CU, WS | Low, also insurance |
| Subtract sound | Munich's phone-bomb scene, no music: Spielberg and Burtt build a noisy bed, single out plot sounds "like beads on a wire", then subtract until the climax is silence ([Nerdwriter 3:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kavxsXhzD48&t=190s)) | The climax lands in what is left out | With locked audio, the tightest size sits on the thinnest audio | Low |
| Withhold the two-shot | Silence of the Lambs: the first two-shot comes when they become a pair ([EFAP 2:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5V-k-p4wzxg&t=122s)). Se7en charts Mills and Somerset through shared frames ([4:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QPAloq5MCUA&t=299s)). Opposite choice, when chemistry matters more than cut flexibility: [The Sustained Two-Shot, 3:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mn2Dcy-NDTw&t=186s) | The shared frame marks the relationship | Singles until the relationship beat, then the first shared frame | Low, avoids multi-character drift until it pays |
| Fast inserts need fewer details | Kon held a bag 6 frames against 10 in a comparable live-action moment, a note 10 against 49, because he drew less ([EFAP 4:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oz49vQwSoTE&t=291s)) | Less detail reads in fewer frames | ~6-10 frames for one clean object on a plain ground, longer for text | Low |

**Safe escalation `[inference]`:** WS moving, MS static, CU static with the action, an axial crop tighter on the peak
with no new generation, a hold, a WS release moving from a new angle. It keeps the camera locked at the action, where
the data shows models are weakest at moving. Size and pace are the two ladders; keep the third, screen direction,
constant until a deliberate reversal.

## Check a generated move with a tracker, not a vision model

Vision-language models confuse zoom with dolly, left with right, translation with rotation, and object movement with
camera movement, Gemini-3-Pro and Qwen3-VL-32B included ([arXiv 2607.03043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03043)). The
best of 24 scored under 60% average accuracy across eight cinematography dimensions ([ShotBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21356)). Never accept a
move on their word `[inference]`:

- Translation and rotation: a geometry estimator (Mega-SaM did better on static and rotation, ViPE on translation).
- Lockstep and focus position: a tracker on the output. Screen direction: optical flow.
- "Follow" shots depend on content; geometry tools struggle ([CameraBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15376)): check by eye.
