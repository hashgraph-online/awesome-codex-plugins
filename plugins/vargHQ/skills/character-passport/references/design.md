# Design a specific person before any picture

A bare prompt ("a woman at a night bus stop, cinematic, moody, 8k, beautiful") hands every choice to the model,
and the model picks its average: a pretty, symmetrical stranger in a new jacket. Design first, in words, then
every visible detail follows from a reason.

## Seven lines per character (in cast/<id>.md, above the passport)

| Line | Example (Vesna, LAST BUS) | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Premise, one sentence | A nurse who holds everyone together all night has nothing left for herself at 2 a.m. | Every later choice has a reason |
| Job and history | ICU nurse, 12-hour night shift, from Split, lives in London | Explains clothes, posture, hands, accent |
| Want and obstacle in this film | Get home; the bus is late | Gives the acting a cause |
| One visible flaw | Pale scar on the chin, tired eyes | Breaks the model's average face |
| One big signature detail | Brows that almost meet; the red umbrella | What a viewer recognises at phone size |
| Silhouette | Long narrow coat, small head, stooped neck | Reads as a black shape before any detail |
| Why each garment is on them | Scrubs under the coat (straight from work); coat old and good (bought once, kept); boots salt-marked (winter commute) | Wear with a reason is the opposite of a costume |

Category characters (grandmas, big guys, deadpan comedians, mascots, beautiful women, fitness, cartoon): open the
matching recipe in varg-character-design; it has the details, clothing and archetypes that spread.

## Silhouette test

Fill the full-body front view with black (any image editor, or threshold the sheet). If you cannot tell this
character from the others in the cast as black shapes, change the outline: coat length, hat, hair volume, posture,
a prop that sticks out. Characters in one film should differ in shape first, colour second, detail last.

## Costume logic

Each garment answers: who bought it, when, with what money, for what weather, and what the day has done to it.
Write one sign of wear per garment (missing button, pilled cuffs, salt marks, shiny knees, a collar softened by
washing). Clothes that are all new are the strongest costume slop tell.

## Slop tells (check every sheet and still)

- **The average face:** symmetrical, smooth, model proportions, no age in the skin. Fix: age as a number, one
  asymmetry, "visible pores, no retouching".
- **Costume without a life:** new, ironed, shop-fresh. Fix: wear per garment.
- **The same smile:** mouth only, teeth showing, eyes untouched. Fix: cause + eye tells (acting.md).
- **Catalogue props:** perfect, centred, weightless. Fix: prop passport with a defect and a motion block (props.md).
- **A face you know:** "rugged", "bald", "stubble" have come back as famous actors on Nano Banana. Re-check every
  face; change a feature if it resembles someone.
- **Value words** in a character line: beautiful, stunning, perfect, flawless, cinematic, masterpiece. Delete them.
- **Generic job, generic name:** "a businessman", "Sarah". A real name from a real place pulls specific features.
- **Tiny identity:** a character recognised only by a 2 cm detail. Small and side-specific details vanished or
  mirrored in our test; identity must rest on big shapes.

## References for design

Real people, not AI images: street portraits, period photos, museum costume collections, occupational photos.
Tested sources and how to search them: reference-sites.md. Take features from several people and mix them; never
copy one real person's face.

## Evidence (sources and quotes in craft-evidence.md)

- Generated faces drift to the middle: 86.5% of generated people had lighter skin and 74% looked young in one study
  ([Dinkar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00749v2)); AI faces are "more average (and therefore less
  distinctive)" and more attractive than real ones ([BPS on Miller et al.](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/ai-faces-seem-realer-real)).
- People spot AI images by "glossy, shiny skin, windswept hair", empty gaze, glitched clothing details, dysfunctional
  objects, fake lettering and an "unnaturally dramatic and cinematic" scene ([Kamali et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08651)).
- Silhouette identifies a character "at first read" ([Valve, TF2](https://cdn.fastly.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2008/GDC2008_StylizationWithAPurpose_TF2.pdf));
  a primary feature should never be removed in any version, and "Having detail everywhere is as good as having no
  detail at all" ([Riot](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/)).
- Costume is history: wear goes where the body acts on the cloth (elbows, knees, seat, cuffs, collar, pocket mouths,
  strap lines, shoe toes and heels), fading on the sun side, dirt in seams. At least 3 explainable wear marks in a
  medium shot.
- No twins: at least two deliberate asymmetries (parting, scar, one rolled sleeve, bag on one side, uneven wear).
