## Why prototype, and why not

- "We'd write a prompt and Seedance would do whatever it wanted. Wrong camera, wrong blocking, ships changing size between shots." [PJ Ace](https://x.com/PJaccetturo/status/2112899205412901276) (101,092 followers, 57 bookmarks)
- "i quite like the idea of procedural code for storyboarding and control, and then video to video models for texturing and looksmaxxing." [Andrej Karpathy](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2084017844455690558) (4,424,125 followers, 33 bookmarks)
- "I’m very wary of working to a storyboard, or certainly to a previs." [Roger Deakins](https://beforesandafters.com/2021/10/25/roger-deakins-on-cinematography-and-vfx-cross-over-and-why-the-dop-calls-his-stills-photography-personal-scribbles/)
- "a composition you absolutely must hit: previz. Some image in mind but not settled: storyboard. B-roll you are happy to leave to the AI: text + references only." [Keigo Matsumaru](https://x.com/k_matsumaru/status/2100535843652075882) (45,543 followers, 57 bookmarks, tr.)
- "Perhaps the order is: say in words what it means and why it matters, and only if that fails does the idea of images, references, and then previz come up." [Int.Lab](https://x.com/IntLab0000/status/2100520370424193430) (1,797 followers, 35 bookmarks, tr.)

- "IMO, that’s a bad idea if you want realism. Lower-fidelity input lets the model translate your intent better." [Andy Orsow](https://x.com/andyorsow/status/2111144232841040280) (10,759 followers, 777 bookmarks)

## Which tool

- "If you are locking down acting, camera, timing and speed, I think you should build it properly in Blender or similar, but that costs the biggest advantage of AI production, the speed to finish. A simple previz in three.js for placement and rough motion, letting the AI model fill in the rest, is easier and, I feel, fits a workflow right away." [@tebasaki3D](https://x.com/tebasaki3D/status/2109849775881470037) (4,925 followers, tr.)
- "The main difference is that Blender has everything mapped accurately in a 3D space where you have exact control of each angle." [Theo Dudley](https://x.com/TheoDudley/status/2113645304755212772) (748 followers)
- "I just need primitives. I don’t need a super high def rendering. I want to block scenes and that’s it. Working in three.js means a web based 3D realtime environment I can manipulate and it’s much faster" [Henry Daubrez](https://x.com/henrydaubrez/status/2109309789088190693) (60,942 followers, 3 bookmarks)
- "Creating it is faster in Three.js than in Blender, but AI inference during scene creation takes most of the time (about 10 minutes). A 30-second previz took about 1:30 in Three.js and about 2:30 in Blender, so creation speed itself wasn't that different." [Keigo Matsumaru](https://x.com/k_matsumaru/status/2096767779907244506) (45,543 followers, 1 bookmarks, tr.)
- "Blender is incredibly powerful, and when combined with AI, it can deliver much more realistic results than Three.js." [Rubens Soto](https://x.com/rubenssoto_ai/status/2116500441273954801) (2,687 followers, 7 bookmarks)
- "the truth is any 3D program with MCP can do it." [Aharon Rabinowitz](https://x.com/ABAOProductions/status/2114005178290495615) (22,118 followers)

## Prompting and geometry

- "I write seconds and distances because the parts you can't write are precisely the parts that are vague in your own head." [Atsufumi Kashima](https://note.com/atsu_mada/n/na01218f008af) (110 followers, tr.)
- "Adjectives are where the default look lives. Every adjective you replace with a number is a decision you take back from the model." [Florence (@flxrnc)](https://x.com/flxrnc/status/2105311836190978392) (3,333 followers, 8,768 bookmarks)
- "The most important thing I learned is that the references carry most of the quality in your prompt." [Leon Lin](https://x.com/LexnLin/status/2116662591363690999) (33,108 followers, 1,067 bookmarks)
- "The images made discrepancies easier to describe. They did not automatically turn the illustrated shapes into the right meshes." [Ben Sun](https://x.com/SunBin70909/status/2115873237330489362) (84 followers)
- "The more detail you put into the previs, the more literally Seedance follows it." [Grand Visions](https://x.com/GrandVisionsAI/status/2096993565599490465) (140 followers, 361 bookmarks)
- "Ambiguity ruins the pass; crudeness does not: a bent lamppost comes back as a snake, loose limb blocks as extra people, an unmarked car driving backward." [Scenario](https://github.com/scenario-labs/skills/blob/main/skills/dcc/blender/scenario-blender-blockout/SKILL.md)
- "I omit rivets, leaves, facial features and clothing wrinkles, and keep direction, joints, openings and contact surfaces." [Cell (@cellinlab)](https://zaowujuzhen.com/articles/yong-dai-ma-pai-yan-ai-shi-pin-cong-hai-dao-deng-lu-dao-yi-dong-ka-che) (35,046 followers, tr.)
- "a "do not add" list. without it the AI keeps adding things nobody asked for." [Prasenjit (@prasenx)](https://x.com/prasenx/status/2115451421147738432) (23,278 followers, 18 bookmarks)
- "if the first result doesn’t get reasonably close to the aesthetic you want, it’s often better to start over with a new prompt rather than keep trying to fix it." [Dilum Sanjaya](https://x.com/DilumSanjaya/status/2089179586911482146) (76,016 followers, 122 bookmarks)
- "Count your follow-ups and post every prompt with the work. Receipts convince people; "one prompt" makes them suspicious." [Florence (@flxrnc)](https://x.com/flxrnc/status/2105311836190978392) (3,333 followers, 8,768 bookmarks)

## Camera and staging, from pros

- "Block it out first without animation. Do a series of static compositions in the same scene file." [fxguide on Chris Edwards's previs guide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=21653)
- "Get accurate lens settings and camera settings. Try and use only lenses that could be used on the real shoot." [fxguide on Chris Edwards's previs guide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=21653)
- "We try not to do anything a real camera couldn’t physically do." [Patrick Lin](https://theasc.com/articles/toy-story-4-creating-a-virtual-cooke-look)
- "But in computer animation, it’s actually camera, action, lights." [Patrick Lin](https://theasc.com/articles/toy-story-4-creating-a-virtual-cooke-look)
- "you find it’s an 18mm, and it’s a close up of an actress or something! It drives me insane" [Roger Deakins](https://beforesandafters.com/2021/10/25/roger-deakins-on-cinematography-and-vfx-cross-over-and-why-the-dop-calls-his-stills-photography-personal-scribbles/)

## Judging with an LLM

- "Simply asking an LLM to score the outputs was not reliable enough, even with a very descriptive evaluation rubric." [Ghita Houir Alami (Notion)](https://x.com/ghita__ha/status/2112924291746734557) (2,982 followers, 116 bookmarks)
- "Shipping an unverified LLM judge is the fastest way for people to lose trust in your evals (and your work in general)." [Hamel Husain](https://x.com/HamelHusain/status/2010782094256222323) (64,797 followers, 98 bookmarks)
- "Reviewers can become so focused on checking each item that they overlook problems outside the rubric." [Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/) (64,797 followers)
- "Aim for about 100 examples per failure mode, with enough Pass and Fail examples to measure both classes." [Hamel Husain](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/llm-judge/) (64,797 followers)
- "For most small to medium-sized companies, appointing a single domain expert as a “benevolent dictator” is the most effective approach." [Hamel Husain and Shreya Shankar](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/) (64,797 followers)
- "Criteria drift raises serious questions for tools that assume the independence of evaluation from regular human observation of model outputs." [Shreya Shankar](https://x.com/sh_reya/status/1782425967115501603) (59,622 followers, 4 bookmarks)
- "The model can review the sheet. It cannot review between the frames, and it cannot review taste" [m4lka](https://x.com/m4lkxx/status/2116504147465757161) (7,244 followers, 302 bookmarks)
- ""It feels off" is a mood. A frame number and a cause is a task" [m4lka](https://x.com/m4lkxx/status/2116504147465757161) (7,244 followers, 302 bookmarks)
- "The separation doesn't immediately eliminate that leniency on its own; the evaluator is still an LLM that is inclined to be generous towards LLM-generated outputs." [Prithvi Rajasekaran (Anthropic Labs)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- "Including phrases like "the best designs are museum quality" pushed designs toward a particular visual convergence" [Prithvi Rajasekaran (Anthropic Labs)](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)
- "One caveat: a bias all the judges share cancels out of these comparisons. Only an outside reference, like human ratings, can measure it." [Towards AI (ToneBench)](https://x.com/towards_AI/status/2117507550081917245) (41,052 followers, 2 bookmarks)
- "I told the judge to find defects, so it finds defects. When the real ones run out, it invents nitpicks and style preferences" [Daniel C. Sunday](https://x.com/DanielChibuihe/status/2117186879493668887) (9 followers)
- "having evals and numbers help us get 80% of the way there faster, but maybe they're a crutch that it makes it harder to polish the final 20%?" [Eugene Yan](https://x.com/eugeneyan/status/1960148508495020234) (31,055 followers, 555 bookmarks)
- "Video and audio judges hallucinate problems, so verify every claim against frames." [Elie Steinbock](https://x.com/elie2222/status/2107186094777434454) (16,801 followers, 539 bookmarks)
- "On a three-minute song clip the fresh reviewer scored every cut 0.5 to 1.5 points above the owner, and stopped moving at about 7.5." [Tal Siach (Take One)](https://github.com/talsiach/take-one/blob/HEAD/references/reviewer-brief.md) (9,258 followers)
- "A clean, simple shape can score 100 here without resembling the photo." [Kaloyan Chernev](https://kaloyan.blog/ai-models-rebuild-a-photo-in-blender)
