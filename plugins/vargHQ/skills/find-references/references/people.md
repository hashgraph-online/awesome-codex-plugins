# People references

A people reference proves who a person really is: what survives once pose, location, expression and clothes are removed.
It does not show how your character should look in a shot, and the picture never goes into a model. The output is an
identity card in words. For anyone who recurs, what comes next (passport, sheet, voice) is the `character-passport` skill.

Firm rules. No brief or deadline changes them. This file is not legal advice.

- **Never feed a stranger's face** to a generator, in any slot, under any name ("style", "character", "ingredient").
- **Never name a real person in a prompt**, including "looks like" a celebrity.
- **Never use a real minor.**
- **Never pass a face to another provider** to get around a face block.
- **Never make intimate content of anyone**, or a deceased performer without the estate's consent.
- **The LLM never infers character from a face.** No morals, intelligence or criminality read off a portrait. Who the
  person is comes from a caption, a record or a person. Typage and any portrait series can slide into physiognomy.

### The identity card

Words only. Nothing here describes the shot. `our proposal`: a synthesis of the sources above, not validated.

| Field | What goes in | Example: fisherman, 62 |
|---|---|---|
| Bones | Head and face shape, brow, jaw, nose including breaks, ears, frame | Long narrow head, heavy brow, nose broken once, big ears, wiry frame |
| History on the body | Sun, scars, teeth, hairline, how the age was lived | Sun-cured neck and forearms, gold molar, missing fingertip |
| Work marks | Calluses, nails, squint lines, a lower shoulder | Rope scars on palms, deep squint lines, rolling walk |
| Carriage | How they stand when nobody is watching | Stands still, feet wide, does not fill silence |
| Biography in one line | Job, place, years, a fact | "Cooperative boat captain, Cambados, 44 years at sea, sons left for Vigo" |
| Never | What this person is not | No model bone symmetry, no veneers, not "weathered" as a costume |

Leave out pose, location, expression, clothes, light, lens, grade and retouching. They belong on other reference lines.
Describe what is visible and what the source states. Mark anything else unknown. Collect 3 to 5 neutral portraits of
different people, 1 or 2 occupational pictures for hands and body, and 1 caption or story.

### The five tests

1. **Strip test.** Neutral portraits. Describe the candidate in ten phrases. Delete pose, place, expression and clothes.
   Fewer than four left means it is a wardrobe or mood reference, not casting.
2. **Swap test.** Would you recognise this person in a different coat, place and mood? Keep only what still identifies
   them. A person answers this one.
3. **Format test.** Neutral portraits. Prefer direct to camera, mouth at rest, plain background. A profile or
   three-quarter adds bones. Occupational pictures are exempt from this and the strip test: they are there for work marks
   and carriage, and people at work look down.
4. **Three-person rule.** A trait taken from real people enters the card only if it appears in three or more different
   people. This builds a type, and it protects against reproducing a real face. A trait you invent for a fictional person
   is fine: write it as a decision and mark it invented, so Bones need not be empty.
5. **Caption test.** Keep references with a job-and-place caption. The caption supplies the history.

## Search it

Search with the cataloguer's job word plus a place or decade. Delete adjectives.

1. Pull out who: job, age, place, one fact of history. Delete pose, scene, expression and clothes.
2. Convert the state to a catalogue word. "Tuesday-night mechanic" is an independent garage worker who works late alone,
   so search `mechanic`, then `garage mechanic` and `repairman` (only `mechanic` was run).
3. Add local words: `fisherman`, `oysterman`, `shrimper`; for Galicia `marinero`, `mariscador`, `percebeiro` (not run).
4. Search period archive, then consented contemporary, curated board, person-run links. Write the card from 3 to 5
   people.

Traps caught in testing:

- FSA teeth, nutrition and clothes belong to the 1930s and 1940s. Take bones, carriage and work marks from it, then pair
  it with one contemporary source.
- LoC walks itself: "[Untitled photo, possibly related to: Portrait of William Giles, miner-farmer of Union Township,
  Pennsylvania]" leads to neighbouring frames of the same sitter. `PH-Evans, W.` on the item leads to the photographer.

Drop `cinematic`, `8k`, `hyper realistic`, `portrait of a` and `weathered`. For the person, use "older adult" or an
exact age ("woman, 74"), not "elderly" ([APA](https://apastyle.apa.org/style-grammar-guidelines/bias-free-language/age),
excerpt). The rest is `our proposal`: a self-described nation or region ("Galician", "Lakota"), not "ethnic" or
"exotic"; facts ("second-shift mechanic, rents his bay, owns his tools"), not "poor"; named marks ("sun damage on neck,
squint lines, broken nose", "one eyelid lower"), not "rugged" or "interesting".

## Rights

Public domain clears only copyright. A person's rights in their likeness are separate. LoC's "No known restrictions"
says nothing about them: its [rights guide](https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/071_fsab.html) lists "Privacy and Publicity
Rights" as a separate field.

| Tag | What belongs here | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `feed` | Your own face. A consenting person with a signed release naming AI use and the intended uses. A generated fictional person with no resemblance to anyone. CC0 or public-domain places and objects with no identifiable person | May go into the model |
| `words` | Any identifiable real person, including public-domain FSA, Commons and 100 Strangers. Portraits by named photographers. Stock faces. Are.na and Pinterest re-posts | Describe it, then use the words. The image never goes in |
| `look` | Pinterest, Cosmos, Savee, Same.energy, books | A person looks. Nobody keeps copies |

The law is moving: see the table at the end of this file.

Some routes ask for an attestation. Venice's Seedance route returns 409 `needs_consent` on a detected face and asks you
to confirm "The likeness in any media you upload is your own, or you have explicit, legal consent from any depicted
individual(s)." ([docs](https://docs.venice.ai/guides/media/seedance-face-consent)). An agent never attests. A person
does, and only when it is true, with a signed release, named uses, named media, duration, the right to withdraw and
representation offered.

## The safe workflow

1. **Reference → words.** Turn the reference images into an identity card. Apply the three-person rule.
2. **Words → generated fictional person.** Generate from the words alone, with no reference image in the call.
3. **Compare by eye.** Check the result against the references and known public figures. If it resembles a real
   individual, change the card and regenerate. Do not run face-search services on strangers.
4. **Lock on a neutral sheet.** That generated person on a neutral sheet is now the `feed` reference. Continue in the
   `character-passport` skill, which turns the card into a passport, a turnaround and a voice.
5. **Disclose.** Follow the New York rule for ads where it applies. In the EU, label anything that would pass as real.

**One still, used once.** Identity card plus a generated fictional person: steps 1, 2, 3 and 5. Three captioned portraits are enough to write the card. No neutral sheet, no
passport. `character-passport` only for someone who recurs.

## Code vs generated

| Method | What it needs | Rights |
|---|---|---|
| Generated (Seedance, Kling, Veo, Nano Banana) | The reference must prove the type. The output is the card, the tagged link list and, later, the locked sheet | No real face appears in any call |
| Code, showing a found picture (HyperFrames, Remotion, three.js) | Keep the record for any picture you show: title, item URL, rights field | No model sees the image, but your film republishes it. In an ad, an identifiable person in an old photograph can still raise publicity rights, so check with counsel or show a place or object |
| Code plus one generated person | The card and one still, as under "One still, used once" | No real face in the call. In an ad a fully fictional human can still trigger New York's disclosure rule and EU Article 50(4): flag it for the person to decide |

## Reject if

- A neutral portrait where fewer than four phrases survive the strip test.
- The source does not say who this is: no job, place or year. Mark it unknown. Do not guess from the face.
- A commercial smile, arms crossed on white, or a stock type.
- Airbrushed or waxy skin. This tests retouching only, never age, tone or body.
- The subject is or may be a minor. Patients and medical records too.
- The board's name contains "AI", or the hit came from a word like `weathered`.
- Every person on the board shares one age and skin tone, with no stated reason.
- Anything in `never.md`.

## Judge it

## The law, for when a person asks

The law is moving. All `reported`; excerpt means seen in a search result. Getty, Shutterstock and Pexels licence pages
returned 403 and stay unread, so check by hand. The UK, China and other places were not researched, and no primary text
was found for Kling, Runway or Veo on real faces.
