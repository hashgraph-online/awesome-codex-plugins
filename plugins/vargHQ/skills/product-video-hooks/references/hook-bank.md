# Hook bank: 70 openers for product videos

Fill the [brackets]. Lines marked with a view count are real hooks that spread on TikTok in 2026 (counts are the original video's views, as reported). Check every line against the platform claim rules before use.

## A. Meta's Hooks Menu (18 types from top-spending accounts)

Shared by Meta's global creative strategy lead (67.7K views, 1,470 bookmarks on X):

1. **Dramatize the problem**: exaggerate the need and the frustration
2. **Reminder**: urgency before key dates (sales, seasons, holidays)
3. **Reaction in action**: the reaction to using or not using it; green-screen react to an ad
4. **Shocking effect**: the product or consumer in an extreme, out-of-norm way
5. **Emphasize the solution**: a quick "magic moment" showing how it works
6. **Satisfying intro**: a sensory action scene with short captions
7. **Motion tricks**: transitions and motion effects
8. **The absurd alternative**: an absurd alternative use and its outcome
9. **Question**: answer a relevant question in the first second
10. **On trend (FOMO)**: people hyped up using it
11. **Negative hook**: open negative, then build the pitch
12. **Destroy / toss & burn**: show what you can destroy
13. **Podcast**: an expert voice pitches it
14. **In real life**: shown in a way people don't expect
15. **Evolution**: how it changes users' lives
16. **Teaser**: intriguing info, but not all of it
17. **Make me laugh**: short, sketchy, native
18. **Skeptical voice**: question the product or the need

## B. Lines that spread in 2026

19. "Been shopping at [store] for 7 years and I'm JUST NOW FINDING THIS???" (29.3M)
20. "How is this legal in [store]?" (3.4M)
21. "You look happier." / "Thanks, I found [product]." (31.1M)
22. "I'll never forget the girl who told me the secret to [result] is so easy." (2.1M)
23. "I could LITERALLY KISS the guy on the subway who showed me THIS." (991K)
24. "Ew, your teeth are yellow." (insecurity hook on a faceless Shop slideshow, 25.6M top post; note: Shop now bans still slideshows)
25. Visual: tight shocked or crying face, hand on head, text at the top, then cut to the product.

## C. Callouts (Hormozi, least to most specific)

26. Label: "[Curly-hair girls], stop scrolling."
27. Yes-question: "Does your [foundation] slide off by 2 pm?" (not on Meta for health conditions)
28. If-then: "If your [dog pulls on walks], watch this."
29. Ridiculous result (non-health): "This $19 pan replaced 6 pans in my kitchen."
30. Scene: show the pain (tossing in bed, jeans that won't button)
31. Likeness: a presenter who looks and sounds like the buyer

## D. Meta's three official hook types

32. Value promise: "Two minutes, salon blowout, no heat damage."
33. Statement of intent: "I'm going to clean this oven in 60 seconds with one product."
34. Question: "What would you do with an extra hour every morning?"

## E. Curiosity and pattern breaks

35. Hidden culprit: "This [everyday thing] is the real reason your [problem]."
36. Attack a rule: "Moisturizing every night is giving you more acne." (only if true)
37. Insider: "What [the industry] won't tell you about [X]."
38. External catalyst: "This new [update] just killed [old way]."
39. Industry enemy: "The [mattress] industry doesn't want you to see this." (opened a reported 57% hook-rate VSL)
40. Open loop: "I didn't believe the [reviews] until this happened."

## F. Negative and testing

41. "Exposing [the $80 version]."
42. "Seeing if [this $12 gadget] is actually worth it."
43. "Stop buying [cheap phone cases] until you see this."
44. "Don't buy another [razor] until you watch this."
45. "I was a hater of [product]. I was wrong."
46. "There's no way this works." (then it does)

## G. Awareness ladder (one product, five hooks)

47. Unaware: "If you have [trigger], your [body/home/routine] has been [problem] all along."
48. Problem-aware: "Why you're still [problem] after [age/effort]."
49. Solution-aware: "Why this replaced my $80-a-month [stack]."
50. Product-aware: "I've tried three [creatines] and none made a difference — until…" (real review language only)
51. Most-aware: "It's back in stock, and it's [real discount] today."

## H. Low-energy "not an ad" openers (UGC realism)

52. "Okay. Last thing, then bed." (already mid-motion)
53. "Core day. Pray for me."
54. "I needed 3 coffees to survive school pickup." (in the car at the school gate)
55. "My back is destroyed after 8 hours at a desk." (then the fix in 3 seconds)

## I. Visual-only first frames

56. The product frozen in a block of ice, smashed open (an AI hoodie ad, 2.3M views)
57. The problem and the fix in one clip, maximum visual gap
58. A rapid montage of failed attempts, then the fix (5.5M views, 57K bookmarks)
59. "App flash": the product on screen for one second inside a bigger video, so comments ask "what is this"
60. A rant in a parked car on a lunch break
61. Filmed "from a lecture seat": a student asks the exact problem, the professor explains
62. Doorbell / "my order arrived"
63. Spin the bottle over options; it lands on the product
64. "Mission: find the perfect [X]… complete!" (magnifying-glass scan, then unboxing)
65. Quick outfit-change transition, then the testimonial (real creator)
66. Group chat: "[Brand] entered the chat."
