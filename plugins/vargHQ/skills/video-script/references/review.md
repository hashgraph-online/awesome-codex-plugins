# Product review

For a creator or UGC review: a short shoppable review (15–60 s, TikTok Shop, Reels) or a long review (5–15 min,
YouTube). A review is a promise of honesty, so most of the rules here are about evidence and disclosure.

## Rules

1. **A review is a preview of owning it.** "The whole point of a product review is to give people a preview of what it
   will be like to own the product. Going through the full experience, highlighting the pros and cons, what it's good
   at, what it's not good at, if I think it's worth it, if I think its competitors are worth it"
   ([Marques Brownlee, Think with Google](https://business.google.com/us/think/search-and-video/marques-brownlee-interview/)).
2. **Give every verdict a frame of reference.** Say what you used before and what you compare against, so "great"
   means something ([MKBHD, 2011](https://rosetta.to/u/mkbhd/how-to-give-an-honest-video-review)).
3. **Prove it with your own test.** Name the 2–3 things that decide this category, how you tested each, and show the
   footage or the number. "Flaws but not dealbreakers" is a section, not a footnote
   ([Wirecutter, Anatomy of a Guide](https://www.nytimes.com/wirecutter/blog/anatomy-of-a-guide/);
   [Google, high quality reviews](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews)).
4. **Disclose at the start, spoken and on screen.** A free product or a paid deal is a material connection; a
   disclosure only at the end or in the caption is likely missed. A review with negatives is still an endorsement
   ([FTC, Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)).
5. **No invented experience.** Only someone who used the product can say "I used it". An AI presenter is an endorser
   too, so it cannot claim use; reviews "by someone who does not exist, such as AI-generated fake reviews" are banned
   ([FTC fake-review rule, 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials);
   [2023 Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)).
   With an AI presenter, write the review as a demonstration of measured facts, or use the real reviewer's words.
6. **Short form: hook on the product in 3–6 s, then the key message, then an easy next step said and shown**
   ([TikTok Shop Academy, via ppc.land](https://ppc.land/tiktok-shops-shoppable-video-content-guide-tells-creators-what-actually-works/)).
   The strongest honest hook is a test result that surprised you.

## Beats: short review (30–60 s)

| Beat | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Hook + disclosure | 0–3 s | the surprising result, product in frame; "#ad / they sent it free" on screen and said |
| Who I am to judge | 3–10 s | how long, how often, what I used before |
| Good, with proof | 10–25 s | 2 things it does well, each shown |
| The flaw | 25–33 s | the real downside, shown |
| Verdict + who it's for | 33–40 s | worth it for whom, not for whom |
| Next step | last 5 s | price, where, said and shown |

## Beats: long review (5–15 min)

Disclosure and how you got it → why trust me and my frame of reference → who it is for → how I tested (the 2–3 deciding
factors) → the experience, good and bad, with footage → verdict against the alternatives → flaws but not dealbreakers →
budget, upgrade and runner-up picks → what to watch for next. MKBHD says he now makes "a lot of nine- to-14 minute
videos".

## Example: Lumo H2 headlamp, 45 s

Brief (fictional product, the reviewer's own test): Lumo H2 headlamp, 350 lumens, rated 5 h on high, the reviewer
measured 4 h 10 min. 78 g, USB-C, red-light mode, $45. The strap stretched by week two. Used on 14 runs in two weeks.
The brand sent it free.

```
format: review
length: 45s
ratio: 9:16
viewer: runners who run before sunrise
promise: an honest two-week test of a $45 headlamp
facts: the reviewer's test notes (brief above)
strategy: test result first
cta: link in bio
```

| # | time | spoken | on screen | visual | sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:04 | "The box says five hours. Mine died at four ten." | #ad · Lumo sent it free / 5 H? 4:10 | headlamp on a desk beside a stopwatch reading 4:10:22 | click, silence |
| 2 | 0:04-0:10 | "I've worn it on fourteen runs. Before this, a cheap AAA lamp." | 14 RUNS | runner at dawn, beam on a wet trail | footsteps, birds |
| 3 | 0:10-0:17 | "It's much brighter, and it doesn't bounce when I run." | 350 LUMENS | split screen: old lamp's dim beam vs the H2; runner jogs in place, beam steady | |
| 4 | 0:17-0:24 | "Seventy-eight grams. Red mode, so I don't blind my dog." | 78 G | lamp on a kitchen scale; red beam on a dog at the door | dog tags jingle |
| 5 | 0:24-0:31 | "The bad part: the strap stretched. By week two I tied a knot in it." | STRAP ✗ | close-up of the knotted strap | |
| 6 | 0:31-0:38 | "So, four hours, not five. That's still longer than my longest run." | VERDICT: yes, under 4 h runs | trail at sunrise, lamp clicks off | |
| 7 | 0:38-0:45 | "It's forty-five dollars. If you run in the dark, I'd buy it. Link's in my bio." | $45 · link in bio | lamp held toward camera, then tossed in a bag | zip |

Why it works: the hook is a measured result that cuts against the brand's claim; the disclosure lands in second one;
there is a frame of reference, proof per claim, a real flaw and a verdict with who it is for; 85 words in 45 s.
