# Ad and UGC ad

For paid video that has to stop a scroll and sell: UGC-style ads, TikTok and Meta ads, YouTube pre-roll. Write 3–5
versions that differ in angle, not in wording: TikTok asks for 3–5 creatives per ad group with "big differences", and
only about 5% of Meta creatives become winners ([TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en);
[Motion, Creative Benchmarks 2026](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/)).

## Pick the opener by what the viewer already knows

Eugene Schwartz's awareness levels (from book notes; the original wording was not checked), mapped to ad formats by
[Dara Denney](https://x.com/DenneyDara/status/1749544455571489259) and to hooks by
[Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics):

| The viewer | Open with | Beats |
|---|---|---|
| knows and wants it | the offer: price, deal, what's new | offer → product + the one best reason → true deadline → action |
| knows you, isn't convinced | proof or a skeptic line ("I thought this was a scam") | doubt → proof shown → the main objection answered → typical result → action |
| shops the category | a side-by-side, or how it works | compare on the one thing others fail at → the mechanism → proof → action |
| feels the problem | the problem in their own words | problem → why it's worse → product as the fix → demo → action |
| feels nothing yet | a story, a confession, a pattern interrupt | a person they recognize → the problem appears → the fix → proof → action |

## Rules

1. **Say who it's for and what they get by 3 s; finish the hook by 6 s.** Ogilvy's 1972 "Grabbers" rule says the same
   ([TikTok creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en);
   [TikTok Creative Codes](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/library/Creative_Codes_ENG.pdf)).
2. **Show the product by 5 s and name it about three times.** Said and shown
   ([Google ABCD](https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/youtube_google_abcd_reference_guide_en.pdf);
   P&G rules in [Ogilvy on Advertising, p. 224](https://ogilvy.relayto.com/e/ogilvy-on-advertising-dq85bunnm5ady/1lRWJl9Y224)).
   Exception: story, street-interview and "yapper" formats hold the product back until it is the answer, but still
   before the halfway mark.
3. **Problem, solution, proof, and the proof is real.** Ogilvy: problem-solution "has always been above average in
   sales results"; an honest on-screen demonstration beats a claim
   ([Ogilvy 1972](https://myersgrell1966.blogspot.com/2021/11/how-to-create-advertising-that-sells.html)).
4. **One message.** "Avoid doing too much in your ad" (ABCD). Build the selling bones first, then add jokes on top, then
   cut what isn't essential, as Dollar Shave Club did ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/amp/wbna49428347)).
5. **Native and a little ugly.** 9:16, real-looking people, lo-fi beats polished
   ([Barry Hott](https://www.practicalecommerce.com/ugly-ads-perform-best-marketer-says); TikTok).
6. **Muted and with sound.** The picture and captions carry the meaning; voice, music and effects make it better.
   Supers repeat the spoken promise (Ogilvy; [Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/want-to-better-video-ads-for-mobile-well-show-you-how)).
7. **One action, said and shown.** Urgency only when it is true (ABCD, TikTok).
8. **Write it like something is happening, not like someone is explaining.** "A dad who can't get off the floor beats a
   voiceover listing ingredients every time" ([@AlessandroLavis](https://x.com/AlessandroLavis/status/2116895808226304390)).
9. **Mine the words.** Transcribe ads that have run for months and the buyers' reviews; reuse the pain in their words,
   never invent it ([@CEO_Vlad](https://x.com/CEO_Vlad/status/2114767235139223589): "open on the most embarrassing
   version of the problem").

Word budget: a 30 s UGC ad carries 60–75 spoken words; practitioners cap it under 85.

**UGC-style means one person talking to camera in their own voice,** not a narrator. If the presenter is AI, keep
the creator voice and the first person, in the present tense: they show it working now ("watch this", "look how
dry"), and never report past use ("I've used it for a month").

## Truth checklist (every ad)

- A "real customer" is a real customer, or the ad says they are actors (FTC 16 CFR 255.2).
- A testimonial of a key result implies it is typical; if not, state the generally expected result.
- No testimonial from someone who does not exist or did not use the product, AI personas included
  ([16 CFR 465](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-465)). An AI presenter demonstrates;
  it never says "I tried it".
- Paid, actor and typical-result disclosures go in the video, early, spoken and on screen.
- Label AI-made people and voices where TikTok and Meta require it. No public-figure likeness without permission.
- Health, weight and beauty ads: no hooks that attack how someone looks, no results in a set time without qualifiers.

## Example: Fold&Go cubes, solution-aware, 20 s (fictional product)

Brief: Fold&Go compression packing cubes (fictional). A second zip squeezes the cube to half its height. $34 for a set
of 3. The viewer already uses packing cubes.

```
format: ad
length: 20s
ratio: 9:16
facts: Fold&Go: second zip halves the cube's height; set of 3 for $34
viewer: travelers who already pack with cubes
promise: the same clothes in half the space
strategy: side-by-side
cta: link below
```

| # | time | spoken | on screen | visual | sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:02 | "Same clothes. Two cubes." | SAME 5 SHIRTS | split screen: two identical piles of five shirts | |
| 2 | 0:02-0:06 | "Regular on the left. Fold and Go on the right." | REGULAR · FOLD&GO | both cubes zipped full, the same height | zip, zip |
| 3 | 0:06-0:10 | "Now the second zip." | | a hand pulls the second zipper on the right cube; it squeezes down | one long zip |
| 4 | 0:10-0:14 | "Half the height. Same five shirts." | ½ THE HEIGHT | a ruler stands beside both cubes | tick |
| 5 | 0:14-0:16 | "So the carry-on closes." | | the carry-on lid shuts over three squeezed cubes | latch click |
| 6 | 0:16-0:20 | "Fold and Go. Three for thirty-four dollars. Link below." | 3 FOR $34 · LINK BELOW | the set of three, a finger taps the link | |

Why it works: the viewer shops the category, so it opens on a fair comparison and sells the mechanism (the second
zip); the product is named by 4 s and three times in all; the proof is shown, not claimed; 37 words in 20 s.
