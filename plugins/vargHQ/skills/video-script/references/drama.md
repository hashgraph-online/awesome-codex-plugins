# Short-form drama and skits

For vertical micro-dramas (episodes of 1–3 minutes, series of 60–100 episodes), story shorts, AI series and comedy
skits. The craft comes from the people who write them: ReelShort, DramaBox, Holywater, Pocket FM writers and
executives, a study of 28 micro-drama writers, and AI-series makers.

## Rules

1. **Open on the conflict. You have 3–5 seconds, about 2–3 lines.** No set-up; start mid-action
   (Asmaa Jamil in [Casting Workbook](https://home.castingworkbook.com/articles/why-vertical-storytelling-is-forcing-hollywood-to-rethink-craft-speed-and-opportunity/);
   Thomas Blakeley, [InkTip](https://www.inktip.com/article_single.php?a_id=259): "No slow set-ups").
2. **One beat per episode: hook, turn, cliffhanger.** Each episode pays off one small emotional climax while the
   series question stays open ([Safaricom micro-drama brief](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/images/Downloads/Micro-Drama-Content-Brief-Final.pdf);
   [Cao et al., 28 writers](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14045v1)).
3. **End on a named button:** revelation, reversal, deadline or intrusion (InkTip).
4. **Every second carries information or emotion** (Shicong Zhu, DramaBox, in
   [PGA Produced By](https://producersguild.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Verticals-Microdrama.pdf)). A 15-second mood
   shot is a quarter of an episode.
5. **Clarity over subtlety: "tell and show".** Stakes said aloud, emotions big, archetypes clear (Zhu). Keep a real
   arc anyway, in fragments: "The character arc still needs to be there" (Jamil).
6. **Run one engine on a proven trope:** a want, a fresh obstacle each episode, a ticking clock, an open question.
   Revenge, hidden identity, rescue and high-society fantasy are the engines that sell (Blakeley;
   [Sixth Tone](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1017576)).
7. **Who wants what, what if they don't get it, why now** for every scene; if the value at stake doesn't change from
   start to end, cut the scene (David Mamet's memo to the writers of The Unit; Robert McKee).
8. **Write the marketing moments first.** The title is the logline; name about five scenes that will be cut into ads
   before writing (Chrissie DeGuzman, PGA; Adrion Trujillo). Test: can one clip tell the story?
9. **Don't spend the series payoff in episode 1.** The viewer may learn the secret at once (the hidden heir, the
   betrayal); the other characters may not. The confrontation in which the villain finds out belongs at the paywall
   or later.
10. **Put the biggest open question just before the paywall.** The first 10–20 episodes are often free and episodes 1–3
   sell the series (Dennis Ruh in [Variety](https://variety.com/2025/tv/markets-festivals/microdramas-waves-film-bazaar-1236591407/); InkTip).
11. **For AI video, write around the tool.** Talking heads, confessionals and reality-show formats render well; fights
    and complex action do not (Neural Viz in [WIRED](https://www.wired.com/story/the-future-of-ai-media-parody-of-the-apocalypse-guy-named-josh/)).
    Write action the model can render: "cold beams of light shot out from his eyes", not "a cold stare" (Phoenix Zhu,
    [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/05/15/1137326/chinese-short-dramas-ai/)). One main
    location, fixed character references, shots of 4–8 s with a time for every line
    ([Google Veo 3.1 guide](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/ultimate-prompting-guide-for-veo-3-1);
    [PJ Ace](https://x.com/PJaccetturo/status/2116160696971088319)).
12. **Dialogue for lip-sync:** short lines, one speaker per shot, faces frontal or three-quarter, a line that turns
    (whisper to shout) split into two shots. Each character has a want and a way of talking; don't let everyone sound
    like the narrator.

## Episode (60–90 s, 9:16)

| Time | Beat | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | Hook | mid-conflict: a face, a shock line or a reveal; the first image is the problem |
| 0:03–0:10 | Orient | who wants what and what is wrong, in 2–3 lines; clothes and props show status |
| 0:10–0:45 | Turn | one beat with one clear turn; an emotional spike every 20–30 s |
| ~0:45–0:55 | Micro-payoff | one small catharsis; the series question stays open |
| last 3–8 s | Button | revelation, reversal, deadline or intrusion; cut on the question |

The second marks between 0:10 and the button are an interpolation; no source gives one grid.

## Series

Logline title → beat outline of every episode (3 beats each) → scripts. Three parts: setup and inciting incident;
rising conflict with betrayals and twists; climax and resolution. Episode 1 states the injustice or secret at once.
After release, read comments and drop-off points: give more screen time to what viewers love, rebuild the episodes
where they leave (the 28-writer study reports +115% likes and +263% shares after expanding a fan-favorite character).

## Skits (UCB "game of the scene")

Base reality (who, what, where) → the first unusual thing, framed so the viewer notices → heighten it and explore it
("if this is true, what else is true?") → stay on the game; no unrelated plot.

## Example: Wrong Number, episode 1, 70 s (fiction, made with AI video)

```
format: drama
length: 70s
ratio: 9:16
wpm: 150
max_shot: 8
viewer: micro-drama viewers who love suspense
promise: a text from a stranger knows June's name and her husband's secret
strategy: but-therefore
cta: none
cast: JUNE (40s, night bus driver, tired, dry), DEREK (her husband, too calm), the TEXTER (only ever text)
locations: a bus cab at night; their kitchen
```

| # | time | spoken | on screen | visual | sound |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00-0:03 | | HE KNOWS. LEAVE TONIGHT. | a phone lights up on a bus dashboard at a red light | buzz |
| 2 | 0:03-0:08 | JUNE: "Wrong number." | | June types one-thumbed, uniform, empty bus behind her | indicator ticking |
| 3 | 0:08-0:13 | | JUNE. DON'T GO HOME. | the reply lands at once; her thumb stops | buzz |
| 4 | 0:13-0:19 | JUNE: [whispers] "Who is this?" | | close-up; she checks the mirror: the bus is empty | engine idle |
| 5 | 0:19-0:25 | | CHECK THE FREEZER. | the light turns green; she doesn't move | horn behind her |
| 6 | 0:25-0:33 | DEREK: "You're early." | | kitchen; Derek at the sink, sleeves rolled, very calm | tap running |
| 7 | 0:33-0:39 | JUNE: "Shift got cut." | | her smile is too wide; her eyes go to the freezer | |
| 8 | 0:39-0:45 | DEREK: "Sit. I'll make tea." | | he turns his back and fills the kettle | kettle starts |
| 9 | 0:45-0:52 | | | she eases the freezer open; under frozen peas, a passport | freezer hum |
| 10 | 0:52-0:58 | JUNE: [breath] "That's me." | ANNA KELLER | close-up: her photo, another name | heartbeat |
| 11 | 0:58-1:04 | DEREK: "I told you not to open that." | | Derek in the doorway with two mugs, no expression | kettle clicks off |
| 12 | 1:04-1:10 | | RUN. | the phone in her hand lights up | buzz, cut to black |

Why it works: the hook is a picture that reads on mute; every beat turns (a wrong number, BUT it knows her name, SO she
goes home, BUT the passport is hers, BUT Derek knew); one line per shot, short enough to lip-sync; two locations; the
button is a revelation and an intrusion together.
