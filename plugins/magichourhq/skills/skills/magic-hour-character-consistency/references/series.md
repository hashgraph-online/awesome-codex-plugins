# Recurring characters, casts and new camera angles

Use this for storyboards, episodes or a cast that must remain recognizable across a series.

> Reuse my approved character and make the next two storyboard frames: finding a seedling, then bringing it home. Keep the face and outfit consistent. Choose the staging, compare both with the original and retain only frames that pass.

## Keep reference roles separate

Use the project's existing character brief. Record only visible, distinguishing traits and accepted reference views. Mark wardrobe as fixed or deliberately changing. A name, seed or lengthy description is not an identity lock.

For a cast, assign each reference to one character by role and visible traits. Check the model's actual input limit. Keep the first interaction simple enough to distinguish the cast; overlapping bodies and similar clothing make swapped traits harder to diagnose. Do not assume a tool supports per-character weights, regional masks or identity training.

For a new angle, ask what becomes newly visible. A front portrait cannot establish the back of a costume or a hidden logo. Use an accepted view or describe the result as a proposed design, rather than claiming preservation of unseen details.

## Review before expanding the series

Read a scene as a change against the anchor: action, pose, setting, camera and permitted wardrobe differences. Start with that change and the critical identity requirements. Add detail to address a specific failure, not because more words must be better.

Compare each output with the original. Count repeatable details such as eyes, antennae, earrings or scarf ends. Distinguish a missing feature from a feature hidden by a prop; if the brief requires it visible, neither passes. Check the character at the actual delivery scale as well as close up.

If a scene fails, repair that scene from the anchor. For video, also inspect occlusions, turns, cuts and the last frame; a correct opening still does not establish temporal consistency. Keep accepted shots and their source views so the next episode does not inherit an unreviewed scene as its identity reference.

**Evidence:** [Nori's two paired scene comparisons](https://github.com/magichourhq/skills/tree/main/examples/nori-character) publish mixed results: extra identity wording helped one scene and introduced visible problems in another. Multi-character interaction, new-angle fidelity and animation remain unverified in that case.
