# Thumbnail direction and editable headlines

Use this for a cover that must communicate at feed size, including a series with repeatable typography and genuinely different candidate concepts.

> Make a cover for my tutorial on keeping this robot consistent in new scenes. Use my actual character. The exact headline is “SAME ROBOT. NEW SCENE.” Choose a clear composition, keep the headline editable and show me the result at full size and small size.

## Choose one visual idea

| What the content actually supports              | Visual direction                              | Avoid                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| A visible transformation                        | Before and after from the real inputs/results | Invented damage, outcomes or a fake comparison              |
| A recognizable subject doing something specific | One dominant subject with one action cue      | Tiny figures surrounded by decorative clutter               |
| A real question or surprising detail            | A close view of the relevant detail           | Fabricated UI, reaction, endorsement or sensational numbers |

Keep the subject and headline in separate readable regions. Use luminance contrast and a clear silhouette before adding outlines or more words. A small background cue is often enough to identify the setting. Do not turn a requested person or product into a generic stand-in.

## Generate, compose and inspect

Generate only the needed visual using the real reference when identity matters. For exact or frequently revised headlines, reserve copy space and compose text with a licensed font and the supplied wording. Keep an editable text source. If no compositor is available, say the editable layout is unfinished; do not imply the MCP has a text-layer tool.

Inspect near the actual feed size. A 160-pixel-wide preview is a useful stress check, not a platform requirement. Can the subject and the first few words be understood without zooming? Simplify a background or enlarge the subject before adding decoration. Check the destination's actual overlays and crop rather than assuming a universal safe area.

If the user requests alternatives, vary one meaningful idea such as close-up versus comparison. Keep the source, title and allowed budget fixed. An agent's visual preference is not a measured click-through advantage. Stop after the requested candidates or the budget ceiling; do not run an automatic search for a “viral” winner.

Deliver the cover, editable headline, small preview and any unresolved fidelity issues. A later headline change should reuse the accepted image and layout rather than generate another robot, face or product.

**Evidence:** the [Nori thumbnail case](https://github.com/magichourhq/skills/tree/main/examples/nori-thumbnail) includes both authenticated MCP outputs, exact prompts, charged credits, the executed composition recipe and small previews. Both runs spelled or supported the requested headline; the observed advantage is simpler composition and editable copy, not a claimed model-wide or click-through win.
