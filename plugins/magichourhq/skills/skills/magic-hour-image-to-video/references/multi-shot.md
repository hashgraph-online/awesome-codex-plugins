# Direct a coherent sequence from approved stills

Use this when the request has more than one visual beat. Plan shots before generating so a short model interval is not asked to perform an entire story.

> Make a short three-shot film of this original robot discovering a greenhouse: arrival, noticing a seedling, then watering it. Keep the same character and visual style. Choose restrained camera movement and edit the shots together. Use my budget, inspect each still before animation and save the individual shots too.

## Turn the story into shots

| Beat      | Still to establish                                              | Motion to request                                                 | Continuity to protect                                     |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Arrival   | Character approaching the greenhouse, destination visible       | One slow approach; a locked camera if the layout must stay stable | Travel direction, clothing and time of day                |
| Discovery | Character and seedling visible without hiding critical identity | A small glance or lean; avoid simultaneous orbit and body turn    | Eyeline, plant location and scale                         |
| Action    | Hand/tool positioned for a plausible interaction                | One watering action with a clear end state                        | Grip, object count, contact and identity during occlusion |

These are example shot decisions, not instructions to insert a robot into other briefs. Infer a similarly small sequence from the user's actual subject and requested length. Select supported durations from the live schema, allowing room to trim around cuts. Do not claim that every model accepts the same intervals or end-frame controls.

## Approve appearance before motion

Derive each still from the same approved identity reference with AI Image Editor; repair composition there first. For a new angle that exposes unseen details, obtain or establish an accepted view. Do not solve a changing face by describing a different face in the motion prompt.

Choose camera movement for a reason. A push-in emphasizes a detail but changes available crop/copy space; a pan reveals context; an orbit reveals unseen geometry and increases the identity burden. Locked framing with one subject or environmental action is often sufficient. Neither a fixed camera nor a longer prompt guarantees stability.

## Finish the sequence

Animate accepted stills, inspect the full shots, then assemble with the available editor. Cut on a compatible action or use a deliberate transition when geometry does not match. Independently generated endpoints are not automatically seamless. Keep screen direction, eyeline and palette coherent; do not force a failed shot to match by degrading accepted ones.

Add the requested soundtrack and exact copy after picture timing is settled. Check the total duration, cuts and final audible word. Save the individual clean shots, assembled file and a short shot order. Count all stills, repairs and video jobs against the existing ceiling.
