# Presenter series and language versions

Use this when a customer needs recurring presenter clips, pronunciation-sensitive speech or several language versions. Each language needs its own reviewed speech and timing.

> Make English and Spanish versions of this approved presenter clip using my portrait and supplied scripts. Keep the full meaning, product names and final sentence. Check pronunciation and timing, caption each final cut and retain clean versions for later edits.

## Lock the words and performance

Read existing scripts, voice choices and pronunciation notes first. If translation is requested, preserve the claims, names, numbers and tone; do not add a stronger promise or shorten the meaning merely to fit a duration. Show a material ambiguity before recording it. If supplied wording is fixed, preserve it exactly.

Use the authorized recording when already accepted. Otherwise inspect the live speech endpoint for supported voice and language behavior. Do not infer a language guarantee or a clone/phoneme control from a preset name. A pronunciation spelling can alter what is said; keep the approved displayed script separately from an authorized pronunciation-adjusted speech input.

Listen before animation. Check names and numbers in context, pace, unwanted stage directions and the complete ending. Without listening or competent language review, label those checks as outstanding. An automatic transcript alone does not establish natural pronunciation.

## Build each timeline from its audio

Measure each accepted recording. Translations can differ in length; do not reuse the English endpoint interval blindly. For talking photo, the chosen interval is in the input audio. For lip sync, verify enough source video exists and check the endpoint's input offsets. Do not loop an active speaking face or silently cut a sentence to fill a mismatch.

For a longer series, split at natural sentence/scene boundaries within live model limits. Keep the accepted voice and portrait; inspect joins for changes in pace, timbre or expression. Generate the first requested segment and evaluate it before committing the remaining budget.

Caption after the final picture and audio. Check names, line breaks, timing, face overlap and the last caption. Keep the clean master, approved script and audio separately. Deliver an editable subtitle file only when it is actually available; a burned-in video is not an SRT.

For a later offer or product-name revision, change the approved script deliberately and regenerate only affected speech and dependent video/captions. A spelling correction in a separate title card should not require regenerating the presenter.

**Evidence limit:** tool schemas and timing requirements have been checked; published authenticated speech, lip-sync, caption and multilingual quality comparisons remain outstanding.
