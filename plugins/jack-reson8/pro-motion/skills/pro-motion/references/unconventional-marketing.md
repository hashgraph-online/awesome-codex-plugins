# Unconventional marketing: mechanisms you can reuse on a new product

**Why this exists:** the user's view is that the surprising part *is* the ad. A launch film alone gets scrolled past; a bus-stop TV that "slid out of its frame", a mascot that died, or a celebrity replying to your tweet gets filmed and passed on. This file turns those cases into **levers** you can pull for any product.

**When to read:** step 1b (campaign brain), for every launch, brand or "make it viral" brief, before writing the audio plan. **Output:** `campaign.md`, copied from `assets/template/campaign.md`, with at least 6 candidate ideas from at least 4 different levers. Ideas the user mentioned count as at most one axis. The ideas are scored, one is picked, and it names the film's role in the campaign.

Case evidence: ✓ means a linked source states the fact (often the brand or agency's own figures). u means second-hand or conflicting. The full research notes are in the session notes folder, and the cases A–I are in `campaign-cases.md`.

---

## The test every idea must pass (score 0–2 each; ship ≥ 8 / 10)
1. **Filmable moment:** is there one 3-second frame a passer-by or user would record? (Skyworth's TV half out of the frame, the Duo funeral post, the Old Spice reply naming you.)
2. **The product causes the surprise:** remove the product and the surprise goes away. (The TV is *too thin*; the Whopper is cheap *because* you're at McDonald's.) A stunt the product doesn't explain only gets views. Turbo AI had 600–700M views with little growth because the clips didn't show the product (founder interview, u).
3. **One-line excuse:** a single sentence that makes the "error" make sense: "Not the installer's fault — the TV is just too thin."
4. **Someone else plays along:** another brand, a celebrity, the platform, the commenters or the rule-maker becomes an actor.
5. **Afterlife loop:** what users make next (reply, remix, template, collect, unlock), not just how many see it.

## Levers (mechanism → steps → cases → template for a new product)

### L1 · The medium malfunctions (media hijack)
A surface with a standard look (billboard frame, splash screen, app icon, camera roll grid, receipt, loading bar, push notification) **goes wrong once**, and the product's key benefit is the reason.
- Steps: list 5 surfaces the audience touches daily → pick one with a recognisable normal state → break it in the direction of the benefit (overflow, slide out, too bright, too fast) → add the one-line excuse → leave both versions in the wild so people put them side by side.
- Cases: **Skyworth wallpaper TV** ✓ (China, Sept 2026, bus shelters: "the billboard isn't broken, the TV is just too thin", [sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1082440971_121124788)); **Lixin "paper TV" bus shelter** (Shenzhen, Sept 2026: a red bus-stop board saying the TV is "too thin, like paper", with workers apparently still installing it; the user confirmed the brand as 立信 Lixin, but no public source names it, so cite it as first-hand); **Specsavers "wrongly installed" billboards** (UK: posters pasted sideways or with the ladder inside — "should've gone to Specsavers", [creativebloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/specsavers-billboards)); **Tubi "Interface Interruption"** (Super Bowl 2023: the TV seems to change channel); **Coinbase bouncing QR** ✓ (Super Bowl 2022: 60 s of a DVD-screensaver QR code, 20M+ hits in a minute, [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2022/02/14/investing/coinbase-qr-code-app)); **McDonald's UK "Camera Rolls"** (2026: real phone camera rolls whose last night photo is McDonald's, shown as billboards).
- Template: "On ___ (surface), ___ (product benefit) makes it ___ (break). Excuse: '___'."

### L2 · Neighbour hijack (dialogue billboards)
Your ad answers the ad, celebrity or event **next to it**. The reply is the content.
- Steps: find a placement next to something everyone already looks at (a celebrity ad, a rival, a landmark) → write one line that turns their message into your benefit → strike within 24 hours → attack a trait, never a trademark.
- Cases: **999 Cold Remedy** ✓ (China, Jan 2026: next to Jackson Yee's ad, "the handsome guy next door… wear more layers", riffing on the long-johns meme); **Audi vs BMW billboard war** (Santa Monica: "Your move, BMW" → "Checkmate" → a blimp, [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/robschwartz/2012/01/29/audi-vs-bmw-the-war-escalates/)); **Burger King next to McDonald's ChatGPT poster** (2023: "and the biggest?", [famouscampaigns](https://www.famouscampaigns.com/2023/06/chatgpt-billboard-triggers-ad-war-between-mcdonalds-burger-king-and-subway/)); **Cotti vs Luckin** (2023: the day after the Moutai latte, "the late-night liquor hurts your liver, the morning porridge feeds your stomach").

### L3 · The restriction becomes the material
A rule stops you showing something, so you obey it **visibly and badly**, and the audience becomes your accomplice.
- Steps: list what you cannot do (banned logo, platform keyword limits, sponsor exclusivity, no faces) → comply theatrically (cover it, black it out, bleep it) so the shape still gives it away → sell the censored version.
- Cases: **Levi's 2026 World Cup** ✓ (FIFA clean-stadium rule: the tarp over the name was pulled into the batwing logo shape, "Welcome to the beautiful [blacked out] Stadium"; 1B+ impressions per [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/416408/world-cup-stunt-gives-levis-more-than-1b-impressions-.html); blacked-out logo T-shirts sold); **Coors Light flashlight** (NFL players can't endorse beer, so Mahomes endorses a Coors Light *flashlight*, u); **Heinz "Coincidence?"** ✓ (2026: rivals' fry boxes look like Heinz's 150-year-old logo; [famouscampaigns](https://www.famouscampaigns.com/2026/06/case-study-heinz-look-familiar/)).

### L4 · Absurd persona (the mascot has a temper)
A character with a **stable bias** (not just a look) reacts to news, rivals and comments. Big moments are events: the mascot dies, goes missing or gets married, and each event comes back to the product.
- Steps: write the persona as a bias in one line ("thinks every photo is a crime scene") → react only to things already trending → stage one event and tie its resolution to a mass user action → let other brands play along in the comments.
- Cases: **Duolingo**: "Duo is dead" ✓ (Feb 2025; revived after the community earned 50B XP; 1.7B impressions per the brand), 5-second Super Bowl "BBL" ad + push to 4M learners, "Bad Bunny 101" (+35% Spanish learners in a week); **Old Spice "The Man Your Man Could Smell Like"** (2010 film, then the response campaign, see L5); **Mixue Snow King** (China: no story, only an absurd persona; joins memes in person); **Wendy's roasts**, **Ryanair TikTok** (jokes about its own fees), **Bar B Q Plaza "Sad Kama-Chan"** (serialised sad mascot).

### L5 · Personal replies at scale (the fastest content line wins)
Turn "a brand ad" into "a celebrity just answered *me*". Each person who gets a reply shares it.
- Steps: open a channel where people post the problem in public → build a line that ships one reply in about 15 minutes (pick → script → shoot/render → post) → reply to accounts with followers first, then ordinary people → agree the guardrails with legal beforehand instead of reviewing each one.
- Case: **Old Spice Response Campaign** ✓ (W+K, July 2010: 186 videos in about 2.5 days on one bathroom set; 4 writers; a system ranked every online mention; teleprompter; many posted 10–15 minutes after the comment was picked; replies to Ellen, Perez Hilton, Alyssa Milano and ordinary people; 65M+ views; Twitter followers +2,700%; sales +125% YoY at the end of July, [D&AD](https://www.dandad.org/insights/awards/old-spice-case-study-insights)).
- Code tie-in: our render pipeline makes a templated reply film in seconds (`?reply=<handle>` + `render-fast.js`), so a "reply wall" is cheap.

### L6 · UGC army (format first, then volume)
Don't shoot an ad; find **one recordable moment** in the product, then pay hundreds of small creators to record it, renewing them on conversions, not views.
- Steps: define a 3-second screen-recordable moment (upload → transformation, the AI's reaction) → the founder or team posts 40+ attempts to find one format that pops → copy the format to 50–100 accounts (by language and city) → tiered pay with the bottom 70–80% cut → every post must show the product doing the job.
- Cases: **Pingo AI** (YC S25: hundreds of micro language creators, $20–$1,000+ per post, keep the top 20–30%; the AI's funny reaction to mistakes is the hook, u numbers); **Turbo AI** (founder posted 40+ TikToks before one hit, then 100+ student accounts reused the format; 100M views before paid ads per Inc.); **Cal AI** ("watch me scan my food", paid on sign-ups, u); **Perfect Diary pyramid** (celebrity → KOL → 70% ordinary-user notes on Xiaohongshu; dated, u).
- Film tie-in: deliver the launch film **plus** a 3-second template version of the moment (9:16, captions burned in, no logo for the first 2 s) that creators can copy.

### L7 · Fake-out-of-home first, real object second
An 8-second CGI clip of an impossible object in a real street ("FOOH") is the teaser. Say in the comments that it's CGI, then build a small real version people can visit and film.
- Cases: **Jacquemus giant Le Bambino bags in Paris** (2023 CGI, ~40M views; real bag cars driven in 2024, [PAPER](https://www.papermag.com/jacquemus-purse-cars)); **Maybelline mascara lashes on the London Tube** (2023 CGI; brand admitted it in the comments; a real 3D lash tram in Manchester followed in 2024); **Shinjuku 3D cat** (2021: a real curved LED screen, not CGI; became a landmark, [soranews24](https://soranews24.com/2021/07/05/giant-cat-appears-on-new-3-d-billboard-outside-shinjuku-station-%E3%80%90video%E3%80%91/)); **Nike × Zheng Qinwen** (the ball "hit across the Huangpu").
- Film tie-in: this is a VFX job our pipeline can do: a phone plate plus a canvas object, with `cutout.swift` for occlusion and `paper`/`extrude`/`toonBall` for the object.

### L8 · Confess the flaw first
Say the complaint before the customers do, in the smallest possible medium, with a promise attached.
- Cases: **KFC "FCK"** ✓ (UK 2018: out of chicken; bucket letters rearranged in two newspapers, 700+ articles, [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2018/02/23/news/kfc-apology-ad-shortage-chicken)); **Moldy Whopper** ✓ (2020: 35 days of decay as proof of no preservatives); **Ryanair** (its own fees as jokes). Watch out: self-mockery that sneers at the audience backfires.

### L9 · The product forces the social act
The product physically or digitally can't be used alone. Covered as family A in `campaign-cases.md`: **Coca-Cola Friendly Twist** (2014, Colombia: a university's first week of term; the cap only opens when two strangers twist it together, so you can't drink without making a friend; one of the user's favourites), Milka Last Square, Milka Neighbor Up. Add a digital version: an in-app action that only completes with a friend.

### L10 · Own one tiny surface completely
Buy one small, exact slot (one splash screen, one wall, one station, one hour) and make the slot's own UI element the idea. Clicking delivers immediately.
- Cases: **Weibo × Coca-Cola**: the Weibo "eye" logo in the splash screen became a refreshing Coke at the afternoon slump; tap → coupon (self-reported reach ~150M, [EssenceMediacom](https://www.essencemediacom.com/en/news/coca-cola-gives-the-iconic-weibo-eye-a-refreshing-break)); **Uber × Coca-Cola Journey Takeover** (the map, icons and trip moments inside one app, [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/411845/coca-cola-takes-an-interactive-ride-with-uber-adve.html)); **HYLO eye drops** (China 2026: rented a whole wall and left it calm, so your eyes could rest); **Mercado Livre field barcode** ✓ (Cannes 2026 Outdoor GP). China note: shake-to-open splash ads are now regulated (mis-trigger complaints), so don't rely on shake.

### L11 · A short window with a mechanic you can only use once
3–9 days and a location or time trigger that disappears afterwards, with screenshotting built in.
- Cases: **Whopper Detour** ✓ (2018: 1¢ Whopper within 600 ft of a McDonald's; 1.5M+ downloads in 9 days; App Store #686 → #1); **Duo's death → 50B XP**; **Robinhood waitlist / Dispo "develops tomorrow"** (family H).

### L12 · The archive is the proof
Old material people already own (old photos, films, receipts) proves the product's value, so the audience goes digging.
- Cases: **IKEA Hidden Tags** (old IKEA furniture spotted in old photos and films); **Spotify Wrapped**.

## Applying it (the workflow `campaign.md` enforces)
1. **Core purpose in one line**, in the brief's own terms (for example "first-time users feel a great photo takes one tap").
2. **Six candidate ideas, at least four levers.** For each: lever · one-line idea · the filmable moment · the one-line excuse · who plays along · the afterlife loop · the 5-point score.
3. **Pick one main and one backup.** Write what the film is *for* in that campaign: the teaser that seeds the stunt, the proof clip after it, the reply template, the creator template, or the in-app moment.
4. **Push it into the plan:** the mechanism goes into `audio-plan.md` §1 (the sound that *is* the mechanism: the Skyworth "slide" whoosh, a Duo-style funeral organ, a reply jingle). The filmable moment becomes a shot-map row, and the structural surprise from `vfx-techniques.md` §6 should echo it (L1 ↔ "the viewer thinks it's broken").
5. **List what only the user can do** (buy media, contact a partner brand, legal, post from brand accounts), once, in the reply.

### Worked example: a photo-editing app for 15–25-year-old women ("a great photo, one tap")
| Lever | Idea | Filmable moment | Excuse |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 medium malfunctions | half of a lightbox poster is a dud photo; the edited half spills over the frame onto the wall | the photo bulging out of the frame | "The frame isn't broken. The photo is just too good." |
| L5 personal replies | "dud photo shelter": people post bad shots, and the brand replies with the edit within the hour, 100+ in 48 h | before/after reply naming the user | "You asked." |
| L4 persona | a snarky photographer mascot who only roasts dud photos and fixes them; it goes missing until users make 100M photos | the mascot's "missing" poster | "He saw your camera roll." |
| L3 restriction | the platform bans app names in posts, so a blacked-out poster says "can't say the name"; the comments guess | the redaction bar shaped like the app icon | "We're not allowed to say." |
| L6 UGC army | the 3-second "same bad selfie → glow-up" format copied to 100 campus accounts, paid on downloads | the transformation frame | "Sis, nothing worked until…" |
| L10 own a surface | one app's splash screen: a bad photo turns great after 3 s; tap opens the app with that photo loaded | the auto-glow-up | "One tap." |
