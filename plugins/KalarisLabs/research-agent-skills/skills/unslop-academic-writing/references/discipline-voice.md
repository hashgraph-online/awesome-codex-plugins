# Discipline voice norms

Human academic voice differs by field. Unslopping should move text toward the norms of the
target discipline and venue, not toward one universal style. Check recent papers in the
target journal: they are the best style guide.

| Field | Person and voice | Hedging | Typical rhythm and features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biomedical / clinical | "We" common; passive acceptable in Methods | Calibrated, tied to design (RCT vs observational) | Structured abstracts; numbers with CIs; reporting-guideline terms (CONSORT, STROBE) |
| Life sciences (Cell/Nature style) | "We", active, results-first subheadings | Moderate, mechanistic claims need causal evidence | Declarative subheadings that state findings; dense figure references |
| Computer science / ML | "We", active, direct | Light; claims tied to benchmarks and ablations | Contribution bullet lists in the introduction (acceptable), precise method names, short sentences |
| Physics / astronomy | "We", active; some passive | Quantified uncertainty (± values, σ) | Equation-driven prose; terse; units always |
| Chemistry / materials | Mixed; passive common in experimental sections | Moderate | Compound numbering, conditions stated exactly |
| Social sciences / psychology | "We"/"I" per venue; APA conventions | Explicit, often theory-framed | Longer paragraphs; literature synthesis; effect sizes in APA format |
| Humanities | "I" common; argumentative | Rhetorical rather than statistical | Long, varied sentences; quotations and close reading; signposting is part of the argument |
| Economics | "We"/"I", active | Identification-strategy language | Precise causal vocabulary (identification, instrument, exogenous) |

## Calibrating to an individual author

From 1-3 pages the author wrote themselves, record:
1. Median sentence length and how often they write very short sentences.
2. Pronoun use ("we", "I", or impersonal).
3. Their transitions (many authors use "But" and "So"; others never do).
4. Terms of art they prefer (e.g. "cohort" vs "sample", "model" vs "architecture").
5. British vs American spelling.

Keep revisions within that profile. Don't "improve" a distinctive but correct voice into generic polish.

## Non-native English writers

Slop removal must not erase correct but non-idiomatic phrasing that carries precise meaning.
Prioritize clarity and grammatical correctness over native-speaker idiom, and keep the author's terminology.
